## Editing notes

- **Transitions:** brass wipe only, 380ms, one per section boundary. Six wipes total.
- **Clear the frame.** Nothing from section N survives into section N+1. This is what makes a
  62-second video feel like it covers a lot.
- **The interface must not use your display font.** Mock UI in Inter, graphics in Archivo. This
  single split is what makes the screens read as real.
- **Don't move the captions down.** Instagram's own UI covers the bottom ~20% and thumbs cover
  more. They sit at 34cqw from the bottom for a reason.
- **Music** starts at 5.6s, on "one block of text" — not on frame one. Silence under the hook.
- **Hard cut at 62.0s.** No outro card, no logo sting. The last frame is the CTA.

## Hook alternatives to A/B test

1. *"Most people use ChatGPT like a search box."* (current — calm, high retention with the visual)
2. *"You're using about 10% of ChatGPT. Here's the other 90%."*
3. *"Stop typing /deep. You don't need any of those commands."*

Test 1 vs 3. They pull different audiences: 1 gets beginners, 3 gets people who already follow
prompt-tip accounts and will argue in the comments — which is its own kind of reach.

## Caption to post

```
ChatGPT already knows ~40 ways to think.
It just doesn't use them unless you ask.

Paste this into Settings → Personalization →
Custom instructions, turn the toggle on, hit Save:

"You are my Universal AI Engine. Automatically
choose the best methods for every request.
I should NOT need to use shortcuts…"

Full text + the 13-minute walkthrough → link in bio.

Honest bit: this isn't a new model and it isn't
software. It's an instruction layer. It makes
ChatGPT think harder, not be right more often.
```

Keep that last paragraph. On this topic the comments section rewards the person who says the
unglamorous part out loud, and it's the thing that separates this from every other
"secret ChatGPT prompt" reel.
