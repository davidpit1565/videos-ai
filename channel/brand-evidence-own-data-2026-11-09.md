## 5. What the audience responded to, what it ignored, what is unknown

Responded to (FACT on views, HYPOTHESIS on cause):
1. A chat AI doing something odd, shown as a scene with a result: 4 of the top 4 (86, 80, 82, 72). The two best are "ChatGPT told" and "ChatGPT caved". Reach from cold non-followers, 94-97%.
2. A pasteable thing to keep (ep 1: 410 views, 5 saves, 12 shares: the only episode that ever got real sharing) and concrete "your device does X" facts (ep 43, 39: 312/307 views, the best of the non-AI-chat families).
3. Everything else sits at the same 150-250 level regardless of topic.

Ignored (FACT): nothing converted. 79 of 85 episodes have zero shares, 10 comments in total, 2 follower rises in the one nine-day window where we have a daily record. n8n and Claude Code (the topics the business plan and the YouTube demand report point at: n8n 233k median searches) are the lowest-viewed families (164 and 203 median) and have no business-lead evidence either. Privacy settings explainers (17 + 11 episodes, the largest block of the channel) never exceeded 312 views. A generic privacy scare did not beat n8n by more than noise; the median gap is 189-194 vs 164 vs 206 overall.

UNKNOWN: retention and watch time per episode (NOT_AVAILABLE in the studio, screenshots for 6); who the followers are and why they followed; whether anybody in the engine-A audience (business owners buying AI systems, EUR 1,500-8,000) has watched a single episode: revenue is 0 and no lead is logged; per-episode follows; comment content; whether family differences beyond the top 4 are real (medians within one quartile of each other, n=4-17 per family); whether 86/80/82 reach was the topic, the hour or the hook (David's own note: ep 86 failed the first-second comprehension test and still won); whether 5 identical-sub-genre winners (72/80/86 are all "ChatGPT behaves oddly") would repeat in a series (ep 73, 78, 79, 81 that followed 72 did 235/282/221/332: no repeat).

## For the brand decision (HYPOTHESIS, not instruction)
- The only audience this data shows is cold, young, international, AI-curious, mostly non-followers, and scene-driven: it matches David's 9.11 audience line ("curious about AI at any level").
- The data does NOT show an audience for n8n/automation business content, and shows nothing about buyers.
- The one family with a repeatable mechanic and the highest ceiling is "what an AI actually does when you push it", but 18% of views come from 4 episodes, so treat it as the best lead, not as a confirmed pillar (CLAUDE.md: a pattern is confirmed after two independent episodes: 80 and 86 are two different mechanics, 72 and 80 the same one).
