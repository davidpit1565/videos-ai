## 3. What I need from you (in this order)

### A. 15 hook lines
The hook is the first words the viewer reads in the first 1-2 seconds. Rules:
- Maximum 5 words, present tense, plain everyday words, no jargon, no hashtags, no emojis unless one emoji clearly helps.
- The surprise or the stake must be in the first 3 words ("ChatGPT begs me." style: subject + odd thing). The viewer must know in ONE second what the video is about (ChatGPT + the odd thing) without hearing anything.
- Make the viewer FEEL it (funny, slightly sad, relatable "this happens to people I know"), not just understand it.
- Use at least 5 different hook types (for example: a bold claim, a confession, a dare to the viewer, a number, a question, a "wait for it" tease, a relationship/ghosting angle, an "AI has feelings" angle). Label the type.
- Do NOT repeat or lightly reword these, they were tried and judged weak or rejected: "ChatGPT begged me.", "I ghost ChatGPT. It begs.", "ChatGPT begs me.", "Ghost your ChatGPT. Watch.", "Try this on your ChatGPT." Do not write "You won't believe", "Wait for it", "Watch till the end" in their plain generic form.
- For each hook give: the exact words, the type, one sentence on why a stranger would stop, one sentence on the weakest point, and a score 0-100 for "would stop scrolling" and 0-100 for "would send to a friend". The scores are your judgement: say so, do not pretend they are measurements.
