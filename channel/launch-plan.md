## 1. Positioning

**Who it's for:** people who already use ChatGPT and feel like they're getting generic answers.
Not developers. Not "AI enthusiasts". People with a job and a laptop.

**What makes it different:** almost every AI account on Instagram is a list of tools. Yours
shows *the setup* — the exact screen, the exact paste, the exact toggle — and then says out loud
what the thing can't do. The honesty beat at the end of every video is your moat, because it's
the one thing the hype accounts can't copy without losing their whole business model.

**The one-line promise:** *Real setups you can copy in under two minutes. Including what breaks.*

## 5. Setup checklists

**Instagram**
1. Sign up with a dedicated email, not your personal one.
2. Switch to a **Creator account** (Settings → Account type → Switch to professional → Creator). This unlocks insights and scheduling.
3. Category: "Digital creator" or "Education".
4. Fill the bio, add the link (use a single landing page, not a link-tree wall).
5. **Before posting anything:** create a Highlight called "Setups". Every Reel gets saved there.
6. Turn on "Save original posts" so you keep clean copies.

**YouTube**
1. Create a **Brand account** channel, not a personal one — this matters if you ever add an editor or sell the channel.
2. Set the handle to match Instagram exactly.
3. Upload banner, avatar, and a watermark (a small brass mark, appears bottom-right on all videos).
4. Set the default upload settings: category **Education**, license standard, comments on with held-for-review.
5. Turn on **"Allow embedding"** and **"Publish to subscriptions feed"**.
6. Create two playlists on day one: *Setups (short)* and *Full walkthroughs*.
7. Add end screens to every video pointing at the previous video — this is the single highest-leverage YouTube setting most people skip.

## 6. Cadence and format system

Two formats, one production pipeline.

| Format | Length | Platform | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Setup** | 45–75s vertical | Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, TikTok | Tuesday + Friday |
| **The Full Walkthrough** | 8–15 min horizontal | YouTube | Tuesday |

The long video comes first. The Reel is cut from it — same script, same footage, tighter. That's
why the production package has both: one recording session feeds a week.

**Every video, both formats, follows the same seven beats:** the problem → the fix → what it
actually is → the setup → the result → the honest limitation → where to get it. That's the format
your audience learns to expect, and format familiarity is what turns viewers into subscribers.

```
3 · Cut the long video             → YouTube, Tuesday
4 · Pull the strongest 60 seconds  → Reel, same day

```

Four pieces of content, one session. The Friday Reel should be the *limitation* beat — it's
counter-programming against every other AI account that week, and it's what people screenshot.

## 9. What to actually watch

Ignore follower count for the first 90 days. Watch these:

- **Saves per view on Reels.** This is the number that predicts everything else. A "paste this" video that people don't save has a copy problem, not a reach problem.
- **Average view duration on YouTube**, specifically where the graph drops. If it drops at the setup section, your setup section is too slow.
- **Comments asking "does this work for X".** These are your next twelve episodes, handed to you.
- **Ratio of Reels → profile visits.** If it's low, your hook is working and your bio isn't.

Do not post daily. Two good setups a week beats seven thin ones, and this format lives or dies on
whether the thing you showed actually works when the viewer tries it.
