Your phone carrier is already screening calls before you answer.

Verizon, AT&T, and T-Mobile all run network-level systems that identify or block suspected spam and scam calls. Verizon Call Filter uses evolving analytics and databases to detect spam and can automatically send high-risk spam to voicemail. AT&T ActiveArmor automatically blocks fraud calls and can label or route spam calls. T-Mobile says its network-level Scam ID automatically labels suspected scam calls "Scam Likely," and Scam Block can stop those calls before they reach the phone.

You can view or manage these protections through each carrier's app — Call Filter, ActiveArmor, or T-Life/Scam Shield — though the network-level protection itself doesn't necessarily depend on the app.

Important limitation: none of these systems catch every unwanted or fraudulent call. STIR/SHAKEN, a separate industry standard, authenticates that a caller ID hasn't been spoofed — it does not determine whether the caller's intent is legitimate.
