## Why this topic

David's direct instruction (2.10.2026), after flagging episode 55's weak AI-relevance
(Amazon's cloud-processing reason is background, not a demoed AI capability — same

**The problem**: not understanding someone who speaks a different language. This is as
close to universal as a problem gets — travel, family members who speak another
language, immigration, a doctor or official who doesn't share your language. It is not a
subgroup topic.

**The AI capability, at the literal center of the claim**: Google Translate's "Live
Translate with headphones" — real-time speech-to-speech translation through any
Bluetooth headphones, powered by Gemini 2.5 Flash Native Audio (Google's voice-first AI
model built specifically for live speech translation). This is not AI mentioned as a
backstory reason — the AI model is the thing doing the translating, live, as the
hook's own claim.

## Verification (live web search, 2.10.2026)

Per CLAUDE.md's standing rule, checked live, not assumed:

- **MacRumors / 9to5Mac / iPhoneinCanada (26.3.2026)**: Google Translate's "Live
  Translate with headphones" expanded from an Android-only beta (US, Mexico, India) to
  iOS, and to more countries, on March 26, 2026. Powered by **Gemini 2.5 Flash Native
  Audio**, described by Google itself as its next-generation voice-first AI model built
  for live speech-to-speech translation — not text translation read aloud, but a model
  that processes and produces live speech directly.
- **Country/language scope, cross-checked across multiple outlets (chromeunboxed,
  alternativeto, gsmarena) and Google's own blog post**: available on iOS and Android in
  the US, India, Mexico, Germany, Spain, France, Nigeria, Italy, the UK, Japan,
  Bangladesh, and Thailand (12 countries) as of the March 2026 expansion. Supports more
  than 70 languages for the live speech-to-speech mode specifically (the Translate app's
  text translation separately covers 100+ languages — the script is careful not to
  conflate the two numbers).
- **The exact current steps, verified directly against Google's own support page**
  (support.google.com/translate/answer/6142474, iOS version, fetched live): open the
  Google Translate app with headphones connected, tap **Live Translate**, choose a mode
  (**Listening** — one-way, needs headphones connected; **Conversation** — two-way,
  turn-taking, works through speakers or headphones; **Text only**), pick the two
  languages, then just talk. The app also shows an on-screen transcript.
- **The real differentiator, checked directly rather than assumed, since it's the
  episode's own contrast beat**: Apple's competing AirPods Live Translation feature is
  hardware-locked (AirPods Pro 3, AirPods Pro 2, or AirPods 4 with ANC, paired with an
  iPhone 15 Pro or newer on iOS 26 — $249+ hardware) and, per alternativeto.net and
  gsmarena, is **not available in the EU at launch** over regulatory restrictions.
  Google's version works with any Bluetooth headphones you already own, on both iPhone
  and Android, and its country list above already includes Germany, France, Italy and
  Spain — EU countries Apple's version excludes. Stated plainly in the script as "any
  headphones you already have," not "every headphone, every country" — the 12-country
  limit is a real limit, said out loud, not hidden.

**Not claimed, because it isn't checkable or isn't true:** that this works everywhere in
the world yet (it doesn't — 12 countries, said in the episode itself), that it replaces
a human interpreter for anything high-stakes (legal, medical — not claimed), or that it
is flawless in practice (not tested live by this channel — the claim is sourced to
Google's own announcement and independent reporting, not a first-hand demo).

## Round 2 — David's direct feedback after watching the shipped v4 render (2.10.2026)

Three notes, all real, all addressed:

1. **Scene 1's headline covered the phone in her hand** — the one meaningful detail in
   that photo (she's actively using it, which is the whole point of the hook). Measured
   the real conflict: the phone (source y≈1070-1170) sat inside the documented
   face-exception text zone (803-1248px). Fixed with a large centered zoom (no pan) on
   `ep56-scene-e-walking.jpg` for both scenes 1 and 5 — a pure zoom from the frame's own
   center (960px) moves content differentially by distance from center, so her face
   (above center) moves up clear of the text while the phone (below center) moves down
   past the platform's own unsafe-bottom line (1248px, already hidden by Instagram's UI).
   Verified by rendering real test frames across the whole scene before touching the
   real pipeline, not assumed from the math alone.
2. **"live" (real-time) read as "live" (to live)** — a genuine homograph mispronunciation,
   not a one-off. Fixed by removing the ambiguous word entirely: "...translate for you,
   live" → "...translate for you, in real time" (also clearer to a viewer with zero
   context, per the standing comprehension rule).
3. **Voice energy — "like Nas Daily," real enthusiasm per word, not flat narration.**
   Raised `build_voice.py`'s `--exaggeration` from the locked default (0.50) to
   0.70-0.78 across most lines (0.7 is the tool's own "animated" point on its 0.3/0.5/0.7
   scale), heaviest on the hook. **David's own standing order on this**: this should
   become a permanent rule for every future episode, but he is withholding final
   confirmation until he actually watches this episode and hears that it reads the way
   he means (real per-word enthusiasm, not just a louder flat read) — remind him of this
   explicitly when this episode is handed over, don't assume approval before then.

**Real, honest finding from raising the energy**: higher exaggeration made two specific
words swallow repeatedly — "something" (line 2) and "languages" (line 3) — across
multiple independent seeds, at multiple energy levels (0.72, 0.60, 0.58), while every
other line cleared fine. Confirmed this was an energy-induced effect, not a pre-existing
profile limitation (the original, pre-energy-boost takes never flagged either word):
pulling just these two lines' exaggeration back down (line 3 to 0.52, near the locked
baseline; line 2 reworded to drop "something" entirely — "...turned any headphones into
something that can..." → "...can now use any headphones to...") cleared both cleanly,
while the rest of the file kept its higher energy. `voice_doctor --deep` and
`check_accent.py` both came back clean after. Full re-render and `check.sh` re-run in
progress; two full viewings to be redone from scratch per the standing rule, since this
is a materially different build (new voice throughout, new framing, reworded line).
