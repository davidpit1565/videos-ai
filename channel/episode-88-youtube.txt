Facebook can look through photos you never posted. Here is how to stop it

Facebook has a feature called Camera roll sharing suggestions. It looks through the photos on your phone and suggests collages and edits. It is opt-in. Meta rolled it out in the EU and UK on 16 April 2026 and later added Bolivia, Colombia, the Dominican Republic, El Salvador, Honduras, Nicaragua, Paraguay and Uruguay. It is in the mobile apps only: Facebook's Help Center says "This feature isn't available on computers".

How to check it: open the Facebook app, tap your profile picture, then Settings and Privacy, then Settings, then scroll to Camera roll sharing suggestions (it may be under Preferences; menu names change by app version). Switch it off if it is on.

Strongest option: block photo access at phone level. iPhone: Settings, Privacy and Security, Photos, Facebook, None. Android: Settings, Apps, Facebook, Permissions, Photos and videos, Don't allow (menus vary by phone brand).

What Meta says: "You must opt in to use this feature, and you can turn it off at any time." and "We don't use photos and videos from your camera roll for ads targeting." Also "We also don't use photos and videos from your camera roll to improve AI at Meta", unless you choose to share them in an AI at Meta feature.

What we do not know: whether this version uploads photos to Meta's servers (Meta's post does not say; a 2025 test in the US and Canada did), and the exact pop-up wording in Europe. The video is drawn, not a recording of a real account.
