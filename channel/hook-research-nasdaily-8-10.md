# Hook research: two Nas Daily reels David sent (8.10.2026)

Source: the two Instagram reels David pasted, downloaded and analysed here (Whisper large-v3 for the
spoken words and their timing, frames at 0 / 0.6 / 1.5 / 3 / 6 s). View counts are not visible, so this
describes HOW they are made, not how well each performed. Two videos is a small sample: observations,
not rules.

## Reel 1 — DXMYGGXjsLE (nasdaily, April 2026, 170 s, the Nas.com $27M raise)

- Voice starts at 0.0 s, first word "This". Spoken: "This guy invested $10 million in my company,
  Nas.com. I did. Who is he and why did he invest?" — 9 words to the first full claim (~5 s), then a
  question that opens a loop (who? why?).
- Picture at frame 0: his face, close, talking to camera. A second person (the "guy") walks into frame at
  ~0.6 s — the viewer's question "who is this guy?" is answered visually a moment later.
- On-screen text: only 2-3 words at a time, large, lower-middle of the frame, the spoken word in yellow
  ("This guy / invested"). At 3 s the number appears as giant text: $10,000,000.
- Pace: ~2.2 words/s (ours: ~2.9-3.1). Length 170 s: long.

## Reel 2 — C-DSoh1OMrz (nasdaily, July 2024, 60 s, a story from a talk)

- Voice starts at 0.0 s: "There was a Hindu priest sitting by the river. He saw a scorpion drown." A story
  opening (a character in a place), not a claim.
- Same text style: 2-3 words at a time ("There was a / Hindu priest"), the key word in yellow; a small
  NAS DAILY mark top-right; face on camera the whole time. ~2.25 words/s.

## What this says about David's "up to three words" idea (facts first)

- The 2-3 words are NOT the spoken hook — the spoken opening is a normal ~9-word sentence. The 2-3 words
  are what is on screen at any one moment (caption chunks that follow the voice).
- Our photo-style episodes (55 onward) show the whole hook as one fixed block of 13-26 words from the
  top of the frame, built word by word. Before episode 55 the captions were 2-3-word chunks (`karaoke.py`).
  So the visible difference from this style is the amount of text on screen at once, not the spoken hook.
- Both reels have a human face from frame 0; ours never show a face (the growth audit's point 4).

## HYPOTHESES (not tested here)

1. A 13-26-word text block takes longer to read than the first 3 seconds allow; 2-3-word chunks that follow
   the voice read instantly. Test: episode 63.
2. A concrete stake plus an open question in the first sentence ("This guy invested $10 million... who is he?")
   pulls harder than a bare statement. Our own best hooks (see content-memory.md) are statements of a real fact,
   so keep that, and add the open loop.
3. A giant single number or word on screen at ~3 s (like "$10,000,000") may land harder than the same number
   inside a sentence. Test with episode 63's key word.

## What the wider search found

Vendor blogs only (no study measures hook length in WORDS). They agree on: strongest idea in the first
3 seconds (some say 1-1.5 s), text readable in the first frame, no logo/intro, and "test several hooks".
Their statistics are unverified. Our own data: ep58's 26-word hook got 314 views, ep59's 13-word hook got 33
(ep59 confounded by posting time and topic) — length in words alone does not explain performance here.
