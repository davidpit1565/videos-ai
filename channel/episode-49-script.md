# Episode 49 — script, ready to produce

## Topic
Google's AI Overviews — the AI-generated summary Google puts above the normal list of
links on a huge share of Google searches (Gemini-powered), now used by more than 2
billion monthly users. Independent analysis (New York Times, reported this year) found
AI Overviews are right roughly 90% of the time — meaning **about 1 in 10 answers is
wrong**, across roughly five trillion searches a year, and Google gives no indication in
the interface of which specific answer is one of the wrong ones. Separately, a Munich
Regional Court ruling (28.5.2026) held that Google can be **directly, legally liable**
for a false statement an AI Overview makes — in that case, an AI Overview invented a
connection between real publishing companies and fraudulent business practices that did
not exist, and Google did not correct it even after a cease-and-desist letter. A related
U.S. defamation claim against Google over an AI Overview also survived a motion to
dismiss on 14.9.2026 (Judge Thomas Durkin, N.D. Ill.).

The payoff/technique: Google's own "Web" search-results filter (a tab under the search
box, sometimes hidden under "More" on a narrow screen) returns the plain, traditional
list of links with the AI Overview stripped out entirely — a real, currently working,
zero-cost way to see the underlying sources instead of the AI's summary of them, on any
single search, every time.

## Verification (live web search, 24.9.2026)
- **New York Times analysis (reported via multiple outlets, 2026)**: AI Overviews are
  correct roughly 90% of the time across an estimated five trillion searches a year —
  meaning tens of millions of wrong answers going out per hour at that scale; more than
  half of "correct" answers were also found not to be fully backed by their cited sources.
- **Munich I Regional Court, ruling reported 28.5.2026** (Library of Congress Global Legal
  Monitor, ACS Information Age, others): held Google can be directly, legally liable for
  false statements an AI Overview generates — in the specific case, the AI Overview
  invented a connection between real publishing companies and fraudulent schemes that did
  not exist; Google did not correct it even after a cease-and-desist letter; the court
  also issued a temporary injunction.
- **U.S. federal case, 14.9.2026** (N.D. Illinois, Judge Thomas Durkin): refused to
  dismiss defamation claims against Google over an AI Overview, finding a plausible case
  that Google could have known the statement was false.
- **The "Web" filter**: confirmed via multiple independent 2026 sources (Tom's Guide,
  SUSO Digital, udm14.com) as a real, currently working Google search-results filter —
  appears as a "Web" tab under the search box (sometimes under "More" on narrow screens),
  strips the AI Overview and returns the traditional list of links, no account setting
  required, works per search.
- **Scope, stated plainly**: this is about Google Search's AI Overviews specifically —
  not a claim about every AI product, every search engine, or every Google product.
