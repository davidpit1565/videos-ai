# What makes short tech/AI/phone/privacy videos go viral: research of 9.11.2026

Scope: read-only research, no Firecrawl. Tools: Exa search/fetch, WebSearch/WebFetch, and `yt-dlp` listings of YouTube hashtag/Shorts feeds (listing only).
Labels: FACT(article) = stated by a news/blog article I read, not seen by me on the platform. FACT(listing) = read from a YouTube listing by `yt-dlp` on 9.11.2026 (view counts are rounded by YouTube). FACT(page) = read on the page itself. UNKNOWN = could not be obtained. HYPOTHESIS = my reading.
Not repeated here (already known, see `hook-research-20-10.md`, `research-2026-11-08-reels.md`, `research-2026-11-01/ref-reels.md`): the YouTube title-feature study (n=9,387), the TikTok ChatGPT corpus (n=43), @ekkobuild, @david_knowsai list structure, @good.ball21 AI-image photo post, ryansterling.

## 0. Hard limits of this pass (read first)

- **I did not see a single TikTok or Instagram page.** WebFetch on TikTok video URLs returned only the title "TikTok - Make Your Day"; Exa fetch of a TikTok URL timed out. A `curl` attempt to read the data embedded in TikTok pages was refused by the permission classifier as bot-check circumvention, and I stopped there (no workaround tried). So every TikTok/Instagram number below is second-hand (article, creator's own post, or vendor library) and is labelled that way.
- **YouTube full-metadata extraction is blocked** ("Sign in to confirm you're not a bot"), so for the Shorts below follower counts, upload dates, exact length and first spoken line are UNKNOWN. Only the rounded view count from the feed listing, the title and (via Exa) the description text are FACT.
- Most viral privacy/phone videos found are **not English-first or not high quality** (Hindi, Spanish, Portuguese voice-over); they prove demand for the topic shape, not a hook style to copy.
- Several of the biggest viral "privacy" videos carry **claims that fact-checkers called wrong or overstated** (section 1, rows 5, 7, 8). A big number is not evidence that the claim is true, and our "never fabricate" rule means we copy structure, never claims.

## 3. Five recurring patterns we do NOT already do

"Already do" is checked against CLAUDE.md and `existing-titles.txt`. Each pattern lists the evidence rows, what is new for us, how to use it in drawn UI + calm cloned voice + no face, and the conflicts with existing episode numbers.

### P1. Explain an annoyance the viewer already lived, with one named toggle ("why did X happen")
- Evidence: rows 1, 2 (silent iPhone alarm, 8M and 2.2M), 6 (mirrored selfies, 1.3M), 15 (storage full, 5.3M). Aubrium: relatable and POV hooks travel furthest in tech reels (33x and 43x followers, versus 21x for curiosity gap and bold claim) (vendor, small groups: 72 and 26 reels).
- New for us: our 86 titles are almost all "your AI is doing something hidden to you" (privacy dread) or "does it agree with you" tests. We have no "this thing that went wrong in your life has a cause" episode. The felt problem is a past event, not a threat.
- How to use: first frame is the viewer's own moment drawn as a phone screen (alarm ringing quietly, a small speaker icon with a low bar), then the cause as one drawn card with the setting name, then the toggle path. Calm voice suits "here is why" better than shock. No face needed.
- Conflicts: 69 (iPhone rewriting notifications), 48 (Apple nudity filter), 45 (carrier call screening), 57 (Google camera live AI): all are Apple/Google feature episodes; none is about an unexplained failure, so overlap is topic family only.
- Risk: the AI substance must be real; an annoyance with no AI in it repeats the episode 55 mistake (260 views, 0 follows).

### P2. Take a scare that is already at millions of views and say what is actually true
- Evidence: rows 5, 7, 8 (4.8M, 146K-like post, 1.6M X post); fact-checkers publishing on the same claims: PolitiFact (20.11.2025), Snopes on WhatsApp Meta AI (17.8.2025, https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/whatsapp-ai-chat-privacy/), EFF on WhatsApp Advanced Chat Privacy (2.9.2025, https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/09/what-whatsapps-advanced-chat-privacy-really-does). Several outlets writing a correction on the same claim is itself evidence of search demand (HYPOTHESIS: I have no search-volume numbers).
- New for us: episode 14 did one claim check and episodes 64-67, 83 and 85 quote a help page, but none starts from "the video everyone is sharing" and names the exact scare in the first words. This also fits `hooks-guide.md` rules (checkable in 30 seconds).
- How to use: first frame the scare as a drawn post card with a neutral label ("Viral claim:"), then the primary source sentence on a second card, then "what it does and does not do", then the one setting. Never copy the creator's footage or claim; cite the source's own wording; if we cannot confirm a part, say so on screen.
- Conflicts: 44 (AI can already read your private messages), 60 (Instagram AI chat feeds ads), 65 (Gmail AI help page), 36, 52, 62, 14.
- Honest limit: the viral videos here are the scares, not the corrections. The reach of correction-style videos is UNKNOWN. This is a test, not a proven pattern.

### P3. A hook of one to five words, shown on screen first (text-first, not a spoken sentence)
- Evidence: Aubrium, 400+ English hooks on viral tech reels (vendor): hooks of 1-4 words travelled 58x followers (16% of hooks), 5-8 words 32x, 9-12 words 11x, 13-20 words 16x; text only (no voice) 33x (38% of reels), spoken only 14x, text and spoken together 13x. Examples: "Forgetting something?" 6.4M, "Exams might be doomed" 6.3M on a 5.4K account. In AI automation the vendor says the 1-4 word hooks went furthest too.
- Caveats: correlation in a library of reels that already went viral; short hooks often ride a striking visual that does the work (HYPOTHESIS). This contradicts our own "hook about 7-12 words" line in CLAUDE.md and ep 86-style first-person sentences. Our first-second rule still applies: the short text must name the product and the topic ("Your iPhone mutes your alarm" is five words).
- How to use: our light base design already has big heavy type; put the hook as the only element in frame 0 with the logo, voice starts on the second line. The cloned voice then does not carry the stop-scroll job, which is useful because David judges the hook voice "calm, capped".
- Conflicts: none by topic; it changes the form of every hook, so run it as one deliberate A/B against the medians in `content-memory.md`, one variable at a time.

### P4. "Free" + a named brand + a count, as a do-this-today win (a positive list, not a warning)
- Evidence: row 10 (4.9M, 2.8M; creator-reported), `david_knowsai` list reel already in the 8.11 file (200K), row 9 ("totally free" inside the hook, 649K, 29K shares); Aubrium: AI tools niche share rate 1.57% (third highest of 96 niches); the 20.10 title study found "free" about x1.34 and a named tool about x1.23 within the same channel (both halves same sign).
- New for us: we have shipped warnings and tests, almost never a "here is what you can use free right now" card list, and our setup-guide rule would make it unusually strong (each item with exact clicks).
- How to use: five or six drawn cards, each with the tool's real logo (standing rule), one line of what it does, one limit stated honestly ("free with a daily cap"), the best one last. We must run every item ourselves before claiming it ("never promise what we did not test"). Needs live verification the day of writing: names and limits change (Google's own pages now call NotebookLM "Gemini Notebook", per casrai.org read 7.11.2026).
- Conflicts: 15 (Gemini fixes your broken formula), 19 (Claude keeps your files), 57 (Google's live camera AI), 56 (headphones translate). Topic overlap moderate, format new.
- Build dependency: running other companies' tools needs real accounts and some screen recording, which David asked us to minimise; flag before choosing this.

### P5. A participation quiz whose answer is withheld, so the video loops and the caption carries the answer
- Evidence: row 14 (blogger-reported: 106,743 views on 134 followers; 12 s; loop; answer in caption), row 14's own account (10 followers, 78,631 views the next day). Also @good.ball21's AI-image post (known from 8.11, 311K likes, but one outlier with copies at 873 and 158 views). Aubrium: skits and stories travel furthest in AI niches (67x and 47x) but those need a person acting, which we do not have (HYPOTHESIS: a drawn two-message "spot it" card is the closest faceless form).
- New for us: our concept checklist asks for "a reason to watch twice" but no episode so far asks the viewer to guess before the reveal; episode 72 is a test the viewer runs afterwards, not one they play during the video.
- How to use: two drawn cards (for example two messages), a 3-second "which one is fake?" beat the viewer answers silently, the reveal at the end with three checks as a saveable card, the full answer text also in the caption. Calm voice reads the question, then is silent for a beat. No face.
- Conflicts: 38, 42, 47 (AI voice and text scams) for the scam topic; 54 (video-call face test) for "spot the fake".
- Honest limit: all evidence is small-account anecdotes plus one blogger; the retention claim (over 100%) is the blogger's, not measured by us.

Patterns I looked at and did not count as new: spoken keyword CTA with a freebie (already noted 8.11, works only if we really deliver); authority-first openings (rows 3, 4, 7) because our "what its help page says" titles already use official sources, though we have not put the credential in the first second; polished versus phone-shot (Aubrium: phone-shot reels travelled 28x versus 22x in tech, vendor) which conflicts with our v4 polish and David's "few screen recordings" rule, so I would only run it as an experiment.

## 4. Five candidates (not in `existing-titles.txt`, not ChatGPT-centred)

Scores are my judgements on the rubric in `score-calibration.md`, not measurements. Per that file the last prediction ran about 13 points above the real views score, so I shaded these down. No blind reader was run (not possible in this session): **none of these reaches the 95/95/90/90 bar David set on 9.11.2026; the honest ceiling I found is about 80.** Views-score uses 50 + 50*ln(views/200)/ln(50): 300 = 55, 600 = 64, 1,000 = 70, 1,500 = 76, 2,500 = 82, 3,000 = 85. The ranges are guesses; our median is 204 and our best is about 1,475.

All five need the standing live check of the product, name and limits before a script is written. Each fits a drawn UI with real logos and no screen recording except where stated.

### C1. "Which of these two texts is a scam? One was written by AI." (pattern P5 + P1)
- Hook, on screen first (about 10 words): "Which text is the scam? One was written by AI."
- First frame: two drawn message bubbles from a generic "bank" (no real brand impersonated; real logos apply only to products we name, such as the phone's Messages icon), subject stated by the headline.
- Beats: (1) question, 3-second silent think beat; (2) both texts are polished, no typos; (3) the old tell (bad spelling) does not work any more: Which? found 54% of 1,235 members use poor grammar to spot scams, and got free ChatGPT and Bard to write convincing phishing texts when the word "phishing" was avoided (Which?, 27.10.2023, https://www.which.co.uk/policy-and-insight/article/chatgpt-and-bard-risk-enabling-new-wave-of-convincing-scams-by-fraudsters-using-ai-which-warns-aoiYw3a5HyCO ); the EBA/ESMA 2026 leaflet and Webroot 15.9.2026 say to judge the request, not the writing; (4) the three checks as one saveable card (is it urgent, is it asking for money or a code, did it arrive on a new channel); (5) reveal which text was the fake; (6) "send this to your mum" tied to a named person; tomorrow line.
