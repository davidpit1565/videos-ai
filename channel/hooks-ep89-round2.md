# Episode 89, hook candidates, round 2 (10.11.2026)

Topic unchanged: the video shows the line "Before you answer, ask me 3 clarifying questions you need." so ChatGPT asks before it writes. The ANGLE changed: round 1 announced a fix; round 2 opens on a moment the viewer already lived, and holds the fix back to second 3-4.

Status: all 30 are HYPOTHESES. Nothing here is measured, and none has been read by the three judges yet. My own expectation is written at the bottom and it is not "90".

Rules applied to every line: spoken hook 4-8 words, present tense, plain English, none of the words prompt / OpenAI / tip / hack / trick / challenge / stop / paste, the fix is NOT said, the laugh or the pain sits in the first 3 words, ChatGPT is said in the line or its real logo is on frame 0, max 3 lines of text on screen, real objects and hands (10.11 look rule: photos, paper, real logo, real screenshots).

Two honesty conditions for the build:
- Any screen that shows "what ChatGPT wrote" (hooks 1-6, 10-12, 20, 27-30) must be OUR OWN real run of a short, vague request, shown as it came out. If the real run does not produce it, the hook is dropped or the picture changes to an obviously staged prop joke (hooks 13-19, 22 are prop jokes by design: nobody thinks ChatGPT holds a tattoo machine).
- No payoff says the answer "is better". The claim we can show is: with the line, it asks three questions first.

Format: (a) spoken first sentence, (b) frame 0, (c) family, (d) what is withheld + payoff, (e) who to send it to.

## My honest top 8 (judgement, not measurement)

1. Number 4, "Dear insert name here. You hit send." The only one built on a thing that truly happens to people and is true without any staging: the bracket left in. Work pain, instantly clear at 40, funny at 28. Weak for the 17-year-old (email).
2. Number 1, "Dear Madam. ChatGPT, that's my mum." Everyone has a mum and a phone; the laugh is in word 2; "finds you well" is the tone people already mock. Works for all three ages.
3. Number 13, "No questions. ChatGPT starts the tattoo." The barber joke with real fear: permanent, on your body, and the picture moves and makes a noise in the head. Strongest for the teen.
4. Number 3, "Sorry for any inconvenience. To your girlfriend." The biggest laugh of the set and the clearest person to send it to. Risk: the line does not say ChatGPT, the logo has to carry it.
5. Number 19, "It's a cat. ChatGPT names it Rex." A cat on frame 0 stops a scroll by itself, and the joke needs zero AI knowledge. Risk: pure joke, low stakes.
6. Number 17, "Ski jacket. Beach trip. Thanks, ChatGPT." Three beats, a clear picture with real objects, and trip planning is a real use.
7. Number 5, "Everyone knows ChatGPT wrote that." The only real fear in the set for the daily user (being caught). Risk: the payoff must not promise it will "sound like you" unless we ran it.
8. Number 23, "Some people get the good ChatGPT." Envy and a real gap; depends fully on two real runs looking different in one second.

Pick-one families: 1/2/3 (wrong-tone message) and 13-19 (prop comparisons).

My expectation, said plainly: these should beat round 1's 74 because none of them announces a fix and each opens on a moment. I do not expect a median of 90 from these judges. Judge B said the topic itself is known to anyone who follows AI creators; a hook that does not sound like a tips reel can lift that ceiling, it cannot remove it. My guess for the best three is high 70s to mid 80s. If round 2 also stops under 85, the honest reading is that the topic, not the wording, is the cap.
