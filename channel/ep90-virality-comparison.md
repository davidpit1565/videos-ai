## 2. Where ep 90 sits on the scale (200 views = 50, 1,000 = 70, 10,000 = 100)

Predicted views: 350-1,200, most likely about 600-700. Score about 64-66 (range 56-72). Hook 70-74, virality 66-68, overall 68-71 (the file's own final7 judgement is 74 / 68 / 71 / ~700; I do not move it up).
That is GUESS; no evidence in the files supports a number above our 1,563 ceiling.

Versus our winners (656-1,563): about equal to the weakest two (80 and 82), likely below 86. Reasons:
- Up: a real recording of a funny, emotional reply (human-feeling AI behaviour is the shared pattern of all three winners); short; clear ending joke; sendable to "someone who ghosts you".
- Down: (1) the game is a 2024 meme; the hooks panel's AI-user judge called it recycled, and viewers who know it get no surprise. (2) Hook "47-day timeout" is cryptic: the cause (what was asked, who timed out whom) is only shown at ~4 s+; the first-second-subject rule is not demonstrably met and no blind reader was run. (3) No voice: all three winners had a spoken product-named first line; ours relies on 3 words of text. (4) The strongest line ("Wait... can I have a second chance?") is spent in the hook frame, so the middle repeats it. (5) Typing at 8-10x and a dark, low-contrast chat: little for the eye on a phone; qa.py flags black frames, frozen reading hold at the end, and a quiet loudness (-25 LUFS). (6) Small account: winners got 94-97% reach from non-followers on a good day, but 1,000+ views is only seen once (86). Reach also depends on posting hour and luck. (7) Nothing the viewer can save (the 4-step setup is in the caption only).
- Not a win either way: one sample of four-ish videos; the 3 winners are the same family, so the shared pattern is a hypothesis.

Versus episode 89 ("This prompt has been going viral"), structure only, no results (89 is unpublished):
- 89 copies a proven structure (hook, prompt 1 card, "even more scary", prompt 2, "Test it out. Go.") and gives the viewer something to run and copy; its screens are DRAWN placeholders, and its open loop is "what will mine say". It needs the viewer's own account to complete the loop and has a voice.
- 90 is the reverse: a real recording, no voice, a closed story (the joke lands in the video), nothing to save, no dare on screen. 89 has a stronger repeatable shape; 90 has stronger proof of realness. Not comparable in results.

## 3. Three changes with the highest expected gain (ranked, all GUESS)

1. Make frame 0 and the first second state the whole situation in plain words, e.g. "I ghost ChatGPT. It begs." (hooks-ep90 panel: the same words on a readable zoomed frame scored 20-28 stop-points above the default) instead of "47-day timeout."; run the blind reader before posting.
2. Add a spoken or on-screen send/try line at the end (e.g. "Send this to someone who ghosts you" on the last frame, for 1-2 s) so the sender gets a named target in the video itself, not only in the caption.
3. Hold back the begging reply as the hook-frame so it is not shown twice: open with the 47-days bubble and typing dots, reveal the reply once; and brighten/zoom the chat so the text is readable on a phone (the hook research found this was most of the score gap).

Smaller: shorten the 6 s end hold; accept that the old-meme risk stays (the "your ChatGPT" caption angle is the only mitigation found).
