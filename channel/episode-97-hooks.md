# Episode 97 hook options (13.11.2026)

Context: "Draw a pelican riding a bicycle." given to four Claude models (Haiku 5.5 smallest, Sonnet 5.5, Opus 5.5, Fable 5.2 newest); four real drawings shown as hidden letters A-D; guess, reveal at the end. David rejected "Guess the newest Claude." and asked for a hook that can score 100.

Scores are my judgement, not a measurement (my scores did not predict views: Spearman -0.23). Rubric: stops scroll in 1 s (30), subject clear in 1 s (25), curiosity/challenge (25), easy for our voice (10), truthful (10).

**Honest ceiling:** none of these reaches 100. The material is low-stakes (a drawn bird, nothing happens to the viewer's own account or phone), so the pull has to come from the game. The best options sit around 85-88. The bigger levers are the frame (one big drawing, not four) and making the task smaller and easier to win (a two-way choice beats a four-way one).

Facts used, and nothing else: four Claude models; same request; a pelican on a bike; one model is the newest, one the smallest; the drawings get more detailed; the viewer can guess; the reveal is at the end.

Voice check on all spoken lines: no TR/THR/DR words (no "draw", "three", "try"); "bike" not "bicycle"; no word ending in a soft -er/-le/-ly where avoidable (flagged where present). "Claude" is written "clawed" by Whisper (accepted before).

## Top 5

1. **#10 "Claude's newest, or its smallest?" (87).** A two-way choice is the easiest task to win in your head in one second, and it sits on ONE big drawing, which fixes "four boxes is boring". Risk: needs the frame-0 drawing to be truly ambiguous (Opus 5.5), or the answer is obvious.
2. **#9 "Which Claude made this pelican?" (86).** The most concrete question: a named product, a named object, one picture, all in five words. Slightly weaker pull than #10 because "which of four" is more work than "this or that".
3. **#2 "Claude's newest is hiding here." (84).** "Hiding" adds a small mystery and turns the screen into a search; subject in word 1. Weaker on clarity: "here" needs the cards on screen to make sense.
4. **#3 "Pick Claude's newest pelican." (83).** Task verb first, product and object both named in four words; very clear. Less surprise than #10 and close to the rejected baseline.
5. **#6 "Claude eye test: spot the newest." (82).** Frames the guess as a test of the viewer (identity stake) without a fake statistic. "Eye test" is plain but could read as a vision test for a split second.

Weak and not recommended: #8, #12, #14 (reasons in the table); #7 brings back side-by-side boxes; #5 is only half true for most viewers.
