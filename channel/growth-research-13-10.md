# Why the channel is not growing — research synthesis (13.10.2026)

David asked for the deepest research so far on why 66 Reels produced ~3 organic followers, and
what strategy would actually make the channel take off. Not "find something different in other
videos". Four researchers ran in parallel on separate questions (Instagram mechanics; growth
case studies in the AI niche; attention/brain science and the Higgsfield predictor; why people
follow). This file is the synthesis. Grades: A = primary/policy/peer-reviewed, B = large
dataset or first-hand with corroboration, C = practitioner/tracker, D = vendor/anecdote.
FACT = our own data or a primary source. INFERENCE = reasoning from facts. UNKNOWN = not found.

## 1. What our own data says (FACT)

- Views per Reel have been flat at roughly 150-230 for 66 episodes, across very different
  content (n8n/agents in August: 400-940; privacy/consumer AI since: 150-200). Changing topics,
  hooks, photos, voice and music did not move the level (content-memory.md, 21.9 and 6.10).
- Reach has almost never exceeded the follower count (~123). Instagram has hardly ever shown
  a Reel to strangers.
- Sends/shares: 0 on 44 of 45 episodes early on, still ~0. Saves 0-1. Comments ~8 in total.
- Retention on the best episode (ep 55): ~65% at 1s, ~40% at 2s, ~25% at 4s, ~12% at 10s,
  3-4% at the end. About 60% leave before the hook sentence finishes.
- ~3 organic follows from ~11,500 views = ~0.03%. The follower rise (94 -> 123) is mostly
  people who know David.

## 2. What the outside research says

**Distribution (A):** A new Reel is shown to a small set of non-followers; if it performs, it
widens. Follower count does not gate this (Instagram Creators blog, 26.2.2025). Mosseri (July
2024): sends-per-reach is "one of the most important signals". No public threshold, audience
size or time window exists. No Instagram text lists "follow" as a ranking objective.

**Conversion (B/C/D):** No published view->follow benchmark exists. The best-documented numbers
come from breakouts: one account got 11,634 follows from a 614,966-view Reel (~1 per 53 views,
~2%); an AI-monk Reel ~1.8%; a talking-head Reel ~0.5%; the often-quoted faceless figure is
1 per 1,000 (unsourced). So "50-60 followers per video" needs ~2,500-3,000 views per video at
2%, or 50,000+ at 0.1%. It is a reach problem, not a profile problem.

**How accounts in this niche actually grew (B-/C, n=9, self-reported, survivorship-biased):**
growth came in jumps from one or a few outlier videos, never from steady accumulation at flat
low views. The common moves: start from what already went viral and rebuild it with their own
content ("idea scraping"), obsess over the first lines, and give a free concrete payoff.
Fastest accounts teach tools/prompts/workflows; plateaued big accounts are news/curation.
Face vs faceless, series vs mixed, tutorial vs news: no controlled data; claims are opinion.
No Hebrew/Israeli AI account with verified growth was found.

**Faceless + synthetic voice (A/B/C):** Instagram's originality text targets reposted third-party
content; its examples of transformative edits include unique text, creative edits and voiceover.
No text addresses stock photos or synthetic voice, and no evidence of suppression was found.
The August 2026 "AI-generated profile" rule covers an AI-generated person, not obviously a
faceless account with a cloned voice (open). Separate from policy: surveys show falling trust
in AI content and a human voice scoring higher on trust (grade B/C, vendor-run studies).

**The brain-response predictor (Higgsfield):** Its page names no model, data or validation. "1,000+
hours of brain scans" matches Meta FAIR's TRIBE v2 headline figure (circumstantial). TRIBE v2
predicts average fMRI activation; its paper makes no claim about views, retention, shares or
follows. The only brain->outcome evidence is small group-level fMRI work (Tong 2020, ~36
people, aggregate YouTube views, signal in the first ~4s). No published link from a software
"virality score" to platform outcomes. Verdict: a cheap second opinion at most; test it on our
own past Reels before trusting it.

## 3. The diagnosis

**The binding constraint is a missing breakout, not content polish.** Every documented grower
got there through outliers; we have had none, because our Reels fail the audition on the signals
that gate reach to strangers:

1. **First-2-seconds retention is the weakest measured link** (FACT: 40% left by 2s). The viewer
   has not heard the hook sentence yet, so the problem is the first frame, the first sound and
   what the viewer understands without sound (INFERENCE).
2. **Nothing makes anyone send it** (FACT: sends ~0). Mosseri's gating signal is at zero on
   every Reel. A privacy/settings explainer needs a named person who should get it (INFERENCE).
3. **No reason to follow after a view is visible** (INFERENCE, weakest evidence): the account has
   no stable promise a stranger can repeat, and a different topic every day. Not proven: no
   study isolates series/promise, and it only matters once reach exists.

**Not supported by evidence, do not act on these as causes:** faceless/synthetic-voice
suppression; "topic switching confuses the classifier"; a fixed retention or view threshold that
unlocks distribution; posting frequency; timing; hashtags; Higgsfield's brain score.

**One structural risk:** the services business sells to Flemish SMEs; a large English-language
Instagram audience is not that buyer (INFERENCE; no direct evidence found either way). Follower
count should not be the KPI for services.

## 4. Strategy that follows (to test, in this order)

A. **Treat each Reel as a lottery ticket for a breakout and raise the odds, instead of polishing.**
   Per Reel: (a) frame-0 understandable with sound off, a real/high-contrast visual, speech at
   t=0, claim in <=6 words; (b) a concrete personal stake ("your phone is doing X right now");
   (c) one built-in reason to send to a specific person.
B. **Idea scraping:** collect the 20 best-performing Reels (100k+) in privacy / "your phone does
   X" / AI-settings, note hook structure, first frame, length, payoff; rebuild with our verified
   facts. This is the single most consistent move among documented growers.
C. **Make the payoff concrete and free:** the exact setting + a keyword CTA ("comment PRIVACY for
   the checklist") on ~10 Reels; compare comments per 1,000 views. Cheap; effect on growth is
   unproven (the only study is vendor-run, correlational).
D. **Test the first seconds in isolation:** same script, two openings; read skip rate, % at
   1s/2s/4s, avg watch time, sends, follows per post. Needs >=4 pairs to mean anything.
E. **Test length and face once:** a 25-30s cut vs the 45-60s version of one topic; one episode
   with David's face in the first 2s. Evidence is conflicting or absent either way.
F. **Fix the profile before more reach:** one-line promise in bio, 3 pinned proof posts; measure
   profile visits -> follows (a generic 13.5% benchmark is unverified; use ours).
G. **Check Account Status** in Instagram (eligibility for recommendation): free, takes a minute.
H. **Higgsfield:** run 10 past Reels and see whether its ranking matches skip rate; only then use.
I. **Services:** measure conversations from Flemish SMEs; do not use follower count.

## 7. Addendum (13.10, later) — reference reel + first niche scan

**Reference reel analysed (DeYZ_aVz-h4, 47s, downloaded and watched in full).** A marketer explains
how a 2-second "pause at the right frame" reel got 13.5M views. Mechanism = a challenge with a
hidden frame that forces pause and replay; hook = result + number + on-screen proof in 0-2s;
builds on an already-viral concept and adds a layer. All figures (30K follows, 29K DMs, 11s average
watch time) are self-reported by someone selling marketing: one case, not a rule. Whether average
watch time can exceed video length on Instagram is UNCONFIRMED (sources conflict on whether
replays count).

**First niche scan (YouTube Shorts, ytsearch, 36 Shorts <=75s with a view count; FACT for this
sample, search-biased, small).** Top by views are plain, concrete "fix/hide/turn off X on
WhatsApp/iPhone/Google" Shorts (hundreds of thousands to 1.7M). Fear-style "your phone is
listening to you" Shorts in the same sample sit at ~5-50 views. HYPOTHESIS: the demand is for a
specific problem the viewer already has plus the exact fix, not for an alarming claim. This only
describes YouTube search intent; Instagram discovery differs. Instagram itself cannot be scanned
from here, so Instagram references must come as links from David.

**Whole-picture plan (everything interacts, nothing is a separate list item):**
1. Each Reel gets ONE built-in device that earns a replay or a send: a "find it" challenge with a
   freeze-able frame, or a named person it should be sent to. Chosen per episode, not optional.
2. Topic is picked from proven demand (concrete problem + exact fix), then the hook is rebuilt
   from a documented viral structure; never copied footage, text, script or voice (copyright and
   Instagram's originality demotion).
3. Frame 0 must be understandable with sound off; result/stake in <=2s.
4. The profile, pinned posts and the "tomorrow" ending give a stranger one stable promise.
5. The tracker must pull follows, sends and skip rate per Reel, otherwise none of the above can be
   judged. Needs David's Insights data meanwhile.
6. Audience fit stays an open question (English reach vs Flemish SME buyers).
No faces (David, 13.10): the face test is dropped; replay/send devices replace it.
