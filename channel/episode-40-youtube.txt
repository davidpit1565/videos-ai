Do you know who's already grading how you drive, every time you get in the car?

Most new cars today have built-in tech that tracks your speed, braking, and location automatically. Automakers have sold that driving data to data broker companies, who build reports insurance companies use to help set your rate — California's Attorney General settled with GM for $12.75 million in May 2026 over exactly this.

One setting in your car's own app can turn that data sharing off.

It won't undo what's already been shared. But you have a real, free right to see your own file — the same way you'd request a credit report.
