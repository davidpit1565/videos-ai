## 4. Hook options (spoken; statement, not a question; surprise in the first 3 words; present tense)
| # | Line | Type | First-3-words surprise | Lint | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | "Seventy times more Claude Code. Not even close." | claim + contradiction | "Seventy times more" | 0 risky | Closest to the reference's promise (more out of your plan), states the subject (Claude Code) by word 5, verdict in a 3-word punch. |
| H2 | "Your twenty-dollar Claude plan won't do hundred-dollar work." | stake on the viewer + contradiction | "Your twenty-dollar" (money, yours) | 0 risky | Mirrors the reference's line 2. Strong personal stake, but no tool is named and "won't" opens on a negative. 9 words. |
| H3 | "Free Claude Code hack, one big catch." | curiosity gap | "Free Claude Code" | 0 risky | Shape of "This free tool..." but "hack" is vague: what kind of hack? Fails the first-second subject test (no "tokens/plan"). |
| H4 | "Seventy times off your Claude bill. Not even close." | claim + contradiction | "Seventy times off" | 0 risky | Same as H1 with money words; "bill" is slightly wrong (Pro is a flat plan, the claim is about usage). |
| H5 | "A free tool, seventy times the Claude Code. Not close." | claim + contradiction | "A free tool" | 0 risky | Keeps "free tool" from the reference but reads clumsy; surprise ("seventy") at words 4-5. |
| H6 | "Seventy times fewer tokens. Not even close." | claim + contradiction | "Seventy times fewer" | 1 risky ("fewer", -ER) | Most literal, but "tokens" is jargon for a cold viewer and "fewer" gets swallowed. Rejected. |

**Pick: H1, "Seventy times more Claude Code. Not even close."** Reasons: the surprise ("Seventy times more") is the first three words; the product is named in the first second; it is the reference's promise in 6 words, so the viral pull of the original claim is kept; "Not even close" is a contradiction a viewer feels right away (you were sold something); it is a statement, present tense, plain words, no jargon. The money line of the reference (H2) becomes line 2, so both of the reference's opening claims are on screen by about 5 s.
Weak spots written down: (a) the hook gives the verdict away, so the open loop that carries the middle is "then what is the real number, and where does 70 come from?", not "is it true?"; (b) "seventy times more Claude Code" is our paraphrase of "up to 70x fewer tokens" (fewer tokens per question means your plan goes further); frame 0 shows the original wording "up to 70x fewer tokens" so the paraphrase is checkable; (c) 9 words in total with the punch: the claim part is 6.
Backup if the blind readers miss the subject: H2 as the hook, H1's numbers on the frame.
