Facebook can look through photos you never posted. Only if you tapped Allow, and here is how to stop it.

The feature is called "Camera roll sharing suggestions". If you tap Allow, it looks through the photos on your phone and suggests collages and edits. It is for people in the EU and UK (plus a few other countries), in the Facebook mobile apps only.

Check it: Facebook app, tap your profile picture, Settings & Privacy, Settings, then Camera roll sharing suggestions (menu names change by app version). If it is on, switch it off.

Strongest option, it blocks Facebook from your photos at phone level:
iPhone: Settings, Privacy & Security, Photos, Facebook, None.
Android: Settings, Apps, Facebook, Permissions, Photos and videos, Don't allow.

What Meta says (newsroom, 16 April 2026): "You must opt in to use this feature, and you can turn it off at any time." and "We don't use photos and videos from your camera roll for ads targeting."

What we do not know: whether this version uploads your photos to Meta's servers (Meta's post does not say), and the exact pop-up wording in Europe.
