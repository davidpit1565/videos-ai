That n8n node you built didn't run once. It ran once for every item you sent it.

That's n8n's real model: every node takes in a list of items, and runs its whole logic once per item on that list. Send an HTTP Request node 40 rows, and it doesn't make one call with 40 rows in it — it makes 40 real calls. That's why a workflow that "ran fine" on 3 test items can quietly rate-limit or blow through a bill the moment it hits 300.

The fix isn't more nodes — it's the Loop Over Items node (officially Split in Batches), grouping items before they hit the expensive step. Once you see it as items flowing through one at a time, not one batch of data, the whole canvas makes sense.
