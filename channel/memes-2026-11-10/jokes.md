# Memes 2026-11-10: jokes, choice, self-score

Both images keep only the SHAPE of the viral two-part "Me with X / Me with Y" format (two labelled parts, one relatable difference, a reason to tag someone). Artwork, layout and joke are our own.

## Image A: candidate jokes (Claude vs ChatGPT, tone, a JOKE and not a test)

1. **"Me: yes or no?" Claude: a thoughtful essay. ChatGPT: a table, then an offer for a bigger table.** Both halves tease a habit users joke about (long careful answers vs formatted answers that end with "want me to make that a table?"). It names no number, no ranking, no model version.
2. "Me with Claude: 'You're absolutely right!' / Me with ChatGPT: 'Great question!'" Rejected: both are flattery lines, so it reads as a claim about which one flatters more, it overlaps with image B (the caving test) and it can be taken as a factual comparison.
3. "Me with Claude: it thinks before it answers / Me with ChatGPT: it answers instantly and sounds sure." Rejected: this reads as a claim about speed and accuracy, which changes by model and version and could be false.

**Chosen: 1.** It is the only one where nothing can be read as "X is better/worse" or as a result: the picture (a lawyer making a speech vs a pushy seller with a hand held up against him) is clearly a caricature, and each side gets one affectionate jab. The long-answer and follow-up-offer habits are things users joke about, but I did NOT verify how common either joke is (no search for it was run); that is a judgement, not a measurement.

Honest self-score, sendability (would someone tag a friend), 0-100, JUDGEMENT NOT MEASUREMENT: **A = 58.** Funny to people who use both; the Claude panel is gentle and a little flattering, so it has less bite, and a viewer who only uses one of them gets half the joke. Predicted views are not given: this is a static post and `channel/score-calibration.md` is calibrated for reels.
