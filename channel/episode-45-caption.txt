Your phone carrier is already screening calls before you answer.

Verizon, AT&T, and T-Mobile all run network-level systems to catch suspected spam and scam calls. T-Mobile says its own network-level "Scam ID" automatically labels a suspicious call "Scam Likely" — and Scam Block can stop it before it ever reaches your phone.

It's not perfect. None of these systems catch every unwanted call — a scammer calling from a real, verified number can still get through.

Each carrier also gives you a way to check it in its own app (Call Filter, ActiveArmor, or Scam Shield/T-Life) — though the protection itself runs on the network, not the app.
