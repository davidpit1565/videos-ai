## What the frames and the structure show (5 differences)

1. **The winners show a real AI doing something to the viewer. The recent reels show drawings about AI.** 86 is real chat output from start to finish. 89 types a real prompt and teases a real answer. Every reel from 93 to 96 is drawn cards and placeholders. The clearest case is 95: its whole promise is "see what ChatGPT draws of you", and the screen shows a hand-drawn stick face labelled "DRAWN EXAMPLE". The payoff never appears.
2. **Topic class has drifted from "do this on your own phone, about you" to "news or explainers about a product".** 94 (Claude Motion: only Team/Enterprise users can try it), 96 (black-hole physics) and 87/88 (tool and settings explainers) all sit in the class with a median of 237. That class has never broken 340.
3. **The frame is half empty.** 86 fills the whole 9:16 frame with a phone and keyboard. 93 to 96 put one small card in the top half, and the bottom 40-50% stays as an empty grid. All four have the same Miki template copied.
4. **The stake is about the viewer in the winners, and about somebody else in most of the rest.** 86 is your secret and your teacher. 89 is what the AI knows about YOU. 94 is "your report" at work, which is weak. 96 is a physics problem, which is nobody's. 93 is the exception (a personal accusation), and it is the one recent reel that beat its class early.
5. **Where it goes flat:** the winners add a new reveal at every beat (86: told, told, refused; 89: prompt, answer loading, "even more scary", the second prompt). 94, 95 and 96 reach the "how / save this" card by about 5-8 s and then just repeat or close. 95 ends at 14 s with no reveal at all.

## What changed on purpose, and what changed by accident

- **On purpose:** we moved to Miki style after 89 (David, 11.11). David asked for new formats instead of chat tests (13.11). We tried news reels as an experiment (13.11). We picked new-feature topics (94, 95-Slides was dropped).
- **By accident:** we copied 89's *look* (drawn cards on a grid) and left out its *engine*: a real prompt on yourself, with a real answer being withheld. The drawn placeholders replaced the real output. The small-card layout spread from episode to episode. The topic slid from "you" to "a product".
- **A HYPOTHESIS to keep in mind:** 89's 18 saves may come from the save-worthy prompt, not from Miki style. Style and save value are confounded in one episode.

## Rules that already exist in CLAUDE.md and were not followed

- Whole frame alive, no wasted space (9.11): broken by 87, 93, 94, 95 and 96.
- Real logos and **real product screenshots** instead of a generic stand-in, plus Roni's "must not look AI / real textures and screenshots" (10.11): 95's drawn "examples" break both.
- Meta AI note (11.11): "keep real-chat openers: reels opening on a real chat keep viewers". No recent reel opens on a real chat.
- "Package it as an everyday thing that is already happening to the viewer" (6.10) and the virality concept test #1 and #6 ("something specific happening to the viewer's own account"; "a claim a stranger can check in 30 s"): broken by 94 (most viewers cannot use it) and 96.
- "Choose what is viral, not what is new" (12.11): 94 and 96 are new-feature or news picks.
- The surprise in the first 3 words (10.11): 88 broke it (this rule was written after 88).
