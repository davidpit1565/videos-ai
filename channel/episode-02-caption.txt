Everyone says "agent." Almost nobody can tell you where the chatbot ends.

It's three steps on a loop:

PLAN — it decides the steps itself. You give it the job, not the method.
ACT — it uses a real tool. Sends, writes, fetches, books. Not a description of a tool.
CHECK — it looks at what came back, then goes again.

That loop is the entire difference.

One test settles it: if it can't act without you, it's a chatbot with a better name.

And the honest part nobody leads with — agents don't fail like chatbots. A chatbot
gives you a bad answer and you move on. An agent fails confidently, halfway through,
having already done part of the work.

That's why your first one should touch something reversible. A draft, not a send.
