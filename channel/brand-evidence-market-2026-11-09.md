What the market tells us (INFERRED from the table, one source per claim above):
1. **Taken:** "AI made simple / plain English" (Harper, CatGPT, Kelly, Celia, Rundown); "prompts and hacks" (chatgptricks, Maverick, the prompt-series accounts); "latest news" (Rundown, eluna); "I test it honestly" (as a sentence).
2. **Weakly built / open:** testing as a visible craft with real failures and numbers, in short video, faceless; none of the seen accounts shows a failed run as the main content (UNKNOWN for accounts I could not frame-check).
3. The two biggest curious-everyone brands that work (CatGPT, Harper) are carried by a **person's point of view** (deadpan skeptic-friend, credentialed translator), not by the tips. Our faceless format has to replace the person with a **consistent method or character of the account itself** (a "lab", a "log"), or it is a tips feed like #8.
4. Saves and sends beat likes for non-follower reach (Kelly Stratton's own numbers; Mosseri secondary reports in research-2026-11-09-viral.md). A brand built on "worth keeping and forwarding" fits.

## 3. Open positions (three candidates)
Constraints from David and Roni used as filters: faceless; TESTS things; shows real results AND failures; later sells AI systems to businesses; curious-at-any-level audience; must reflect "visual identity NOT the typical Claude look" (Roni, 9.11). **Conflict to flag:** CLAUDE.md (1.11.2026) currently fixes one series identity: light page, black heavy type, lime/product accent. Roni's feedback and the market (our own light-drawn-UI is the look of arturrioni, auraguy, breakingbreach, cookin.code: see table) argue for a change. That change needs David's decision; all three candidates below keep the product's real logo/colour as a visible element (so the "real logo on screen" rule still holds) but change base, type and motion.

### Candidate A: "Pass / Fail" (a testing lab that publishes the verdict)
- **Promise sentence:** "I run the AI thing you keep hearing about, once, on camera-less screen, and tell you PASS or FAIL in 30 seconds."
- **Pillars:** (1) One claim from the internet, run live, with a stamped verdict; (2) "The failure file": the runs that broke, with the exact error and the cost in minutes; (3) The retest: a claim from an earlier episode re-run after an update (verdict changes).
- **Voice:** calm, dry, slightly amused, plain words; says "it failed" without apology; one number per episode. (David's cloned voice already fits calm.)
- **Visual identity direction (not Claude look):** hard black/white "lab sheet" base with a single signal red and a single go-green used ONLY for verdicts; a monospaced or grotesque type pair (e.g. a heavy grotesque for verdicts, a mono for data; fonts chosen by David from a shortlist, NOT a serif+cream editorial); a rubber-stamp slam for PASS/FAIL with a real sound effect; hard cuts, subtle zoom on the number, a ticking timer; the product's real logo always on screen.
- **Why distinct:** the "I test it" sentence is saturated but a locked verdict system (stamp, numbered log, retests) is a recognisable format nobody seen uses; failures as the headline is the opposite of the giveaway accounts. It carries straight into business-model.html's "publish what broke".
- **Risk:** a negative frame can read as a skeptic account and cap sendability; every episode needs a real run (cost/time, one real run at a time); "Pass/Fail" invites arguments about test design (we must show the method in one line).
- **Evidence still missing:** whether a FAIL verdict gets more or fewer saves/sends than a PASS (we have no data); whether faceless "lab" voice can hold attention without a person (CatGPT/Harper succeed because of a person); any watch-time data for our format (NOT_AVAILABLE per CLAUDE.md).

### Candidate B: "Wait, it can do that?" (one real discovery, shown with proof, for any level)
- **Promise sentence:** "One thing AI can do that you did not know, shown working, in under 30 seconds; copy it today."
- **Pillars:** (1) The hidden capability (a feature or use nobody mentions, shown on the real screen); (2) The everyday swap (a thing you do daily done by AI, with the saved minutes measured); (3) The "try it now" card (the one prompt/setting to save, mirrored on the site /e/N).
- **Voice:** curious friend, plain, "I did not know this either", one explosive first line; no hype words like "killed/insane".
- **Visual identity direction:** high-energy "paper-and-ink" editorial collage per Roni's Vox references but with OUR palette, e.g. saturated cobalt or hot magenta blocks with off-white cut-outs, heavy condensed display type for titles with word-by-word kinetic builds, torn/cut-out layers with slow parallax, grain; real UI screens set into the collage like physical cards; layered sound design (paper, tick, whoosh). Not cream/orange/serif.
- **Why distinct:** the audience promise is the broadest (matches David's definition exactly) but the proof-first rule ("shown working") separates it from chatgptricks-style prompt lists; faceless collage with real screens is rare among curious-AI accounts (most use talking heads: Harper, Kelly, Celia, CatGPT).
- **Risk:** the most crowded lane (position #8 above); without a point of view it becomes another tips feed (engagement 0.12%-0.8% accounts); Vox/paper-cut look is already used by @sanji.chien's reels (FACT), so it can read as a copy; business link is weakest (consumer tips do not sell systems).
- **Evidence still missing:** which discoveries our audience has not seen (a topic-demand check per episode); whether paper-cut style lifts retention for us (untested); whether broad tips convert any viewer toward the business (our 94-97% non-followers suggest not yet).

### Candidate C: "The Build Log" (the business seen from the inside: real systems, real numbers, real breakages)
- **Promise sentence:** "Every week I build one AI system a real business could use, and show the number it saved and the thing that broke."
- **Pillars:** (1) The build (one system, started and finished on screen, time and cost shown); (2) The break (what failed, what it cost, the fix); (3) The number (one measured result: minutes saved, errors, price per run, each marked measured or ASSUMPTION).
- **Voice:** a builder's diary: specific, a little understated, numbers first; "here is what I would charge for this" occasionally (the business-model's "only one who writes a price" point).
- **Visual identity direction:** "blueprint/work-order" look: deep ink-blue or graphite background (not the light page), cream and one acid-yellow highlight only for the metric, typewriter or technical-grotesque type with big numerals, ticket/receipt/invoice-style cards that tear off the strip, a running build counter in the corner ("Build 007"), mechanical sound design (relay clicks, printer). Real tool logos on the cards.
- **Why distinct:** it is the only candidate that is literally the business model: each build is also a case study and a sales asset; the seen accounts that sell to businesses (arturrioni, adamstewartmarketing) sell via DM gates and promises, not by publishing receipts and failures (INFERRED). Flemish version for clients is the same series with local-business cases.
- **Risk:** narrowest audience on the "curious at any level" axis; building takes real time per episode; no client cases exist yet (business-model.html: no clients, only our own system as case 1; we must never invent one); "build log" can drift into developer-only talk and fail the "anyone understands from second one" rule.
- **Evidence still missing:** whether non-technical curious viewers watch a build; whether any local business owner finds us through such videos (zero leads data so far); whether the numbers we can honestly show (our own pipeline) are interesting enough to repeat weekly.

### How the three combine (INFERRED, not proven)
A is a format (verdict), B is a topic promise (discovery), C is a business story (build). A realistic structure: the account's public promise is A or B (reach engine), C lives as a weekly "build log" series feeding the Flemish sales channel. I do not recommend running all three as equals: it re-creates the "not focused enough" diagnosis (David, 9.11).

## 5. Suggested next evidence step (cheap, before David picks)
Run each candidate as a one-minute blind pitch to the panel/Roni with two frames and one promise sentence, then pick one to test for 3 weeks with a pre-set success threshold (views per episode, saves+sends per view, followers per 1,000 views) logged in channel/experiments.md. Do not rebuild the identity before that.
