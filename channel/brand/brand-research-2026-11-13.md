## PART 2. What makes a profile mark look professional at Instagram sizes

### 2.1 The sizes
- Instagram publishes no profile-picture dimensions. Third-party guides agree on: shown as a circle, about 110 px on the phone profile page, about 180 px on desktop web, stored at 320 x 320; upload 1080 x 1080 and keep the subject in the centre. https://postfa.st/sizes/instagram/profile (states plainly that these are not official Instagram figures), https://www.adflint.com/sizes/instagram-profile-picture
- In the feed the picture is scaled to about 40 px (G2, 2018, old but the only figure found) https://learn.g2.com/instagram-profile-picture-size . The task brief's "32-40 px next to comments" is consistent with this; the exact comment-avatar size is UNVERIFIED.
- A circle cuts about 21% of the square (1 - pi/4); only the central 70% square is always visible (geometry, computed on the AdFlint page above).

### 2.2 Eight principles (each with a source)
1. **A logo identifies, it does not explain.** "A logo is not communication. A logo is identification." It must be appropriate, distinctive and memorable, and simple. Sagi Haviv (Chermayeff & Geismar & Haviv), class transcript https://www.skillshare.com/en/classes/designing-brand-symbols-the-principles-and-process-of-making-logos-that-last/404415631 ; also https://thefutur.com/content/what-makes-a-logo-great-with-sagi-haviv
2. **The doodle test.** Distinctive means "you see it once or twice, you can describe it to somebody or doodle it on a piece of paper" (same Haviv source).
3. **Simple and distinctive pull against each other.** "The more simple you make something, the less distinctive it's going to become and more generic." The task is "how simple can you make it without becoming generic" (same Haviv source). A plain check mark sits at the generic end.
4. **The concept must be original**; Haviv's fourth criterion https://www.smh.com.au/world/how-to-choose-a-winning-logo-20150507-ggwce1.html . And a symbol needs a reason to exist: with no reason, a wordmark alone is better, because "a symbol is something that people have to learn" (Skillshare source above).
5. **Design the small version separately; do not shrink the big one.** Each variant "should be designed, not derived" https://strynal.com/blog/responsive-logo-design/ ; detail reduction, thicker strokes, filled instead of outlined shapes at small sizes https://www.viget.com/articles/responsive-logos-part-1-tips-for-adapting-logos-for-small-screens
6. **One idea, one shape, no text, heavy strokes, flat contrast.** "Keep one idea... Two ideas compete at 32 pixels"; remove text; thicken strokes; two flat colours, no gradients or shadows https://malbardesign.com/logo-for-a-small-circle-favicons-app-icons-avatars/ . "Silhouette beats detail" https://designlogo.uk/make-your-logo-work-in-a-3-second-scroll-thumbnail-first-des (commercial design blogs: sound practice, low authority).
7. **Build for the circle.** Keep the core shape within about 70% of the diameter, check at 40 px and 24 px https://www.berger.team/en/branding/adaptive-logos-markenidentitaet-fuer-variable-digitale-touchpoints/ ; avoid a background close to the page colour, or the circle edge disappears in light or dark mode https://www.adflint.com/sizes/instagram-profile-picture
8. **Signs a mark needs simplifying:** more than two stroke weights in the symbol, negative space that disappears at small size, gradients https://strynal.com/blog/responsive-logo-design/ . Test: greyscale, arm's length, real size not zoomed https://malbardesign.com/logo-for-a-small-circle-favicons-app-icons-avatars/

### 2.4 Why the four v1 drafts read as amateur (JUDGEMENT, from `channel/brand/logo-v1/sheet.png`)

Shared problems
- **The idea is "it works = check mark".** That is the first idea anyone has, and the glyph itself is the standard rounded check found in every icon set. Against Haviv's test (section 2.2, points 3 and 4) it is simple but generic: nothing in it can be owned.
- **Nothing was drawn.** Every element is a stock primitive: rounded square, pill, circle, dotted ring, default check, default bold sans. A professional mark has at least one decision no one else would make.
- **No link to the name's letters or sound.** "Actually" (the more unusual and ownable word) is ignored; all four illustrate only "works".
- **A check in a circle next to a username looks like a verification badge.** Viewers may read it as a fake "verified" tick. (Whether any platform rule covers this: UNVERIFIED; the confusion risk alone is enough.)
- **The wordmark at the bottom** is default heavy grotesk, tightly tracked, with the check pasted after it at a different stroke weight and with round ends against square-cut letters. The check is not part of the lettering, it is an emoji placed next to it.

Per draft
- **A, check card:** it is the icon of a to-do or checkbox app. The grey offset "shadow" adds a third tone that turns into a smudge at comment size, and the offset-shadow look is a current web-design fashion that will date quickly.
- **B, aw letters:** "aw" is a word (a sound of pity or disappointment), which is the opposite of the brand. The letters are typed, not drawn; the underline has no reason; lowercase rounded forms are calm, not exciting.
- **C, switch on:** a 2:1 horizontal object inside a circle fills roughly a third of the area, so it is the smallest and weakest of the four at 32 px. The check inside the knob is about 4 px there and disappears. It is a UI component, the exact "AI/Claude look" Roni warned about.
- **D, tested stamp:** three concentric elements (ring, dotted ring, check); the dotted ring vanishes at small size and what is left is the generic "approved/verified" seal of stock sites. The closest of the four to a verification badge.

What was right in v1: black and off-white only; filled shapes; the test at real small sizes on the sheet itself. Keep those.

## PART 3. Three logo concepts (described to be drawn; nothing is drawn yet)

Brief derived from the research: ownable (not a plain check), exciting, black/white + one accent that is not orange, not "AI-generic" (so: no sparkles, no starbursts, no gradients, no robots, no chat bubbles, no brain/circuit, no rounded-square app tile). The mark must do the job a face does for the four references.

### Accent colour, shared by all three
**Signal yellow `#FFE600`.** Reasons (JUDGEMENT):
- It is the only strong hue not already owned by the AI products the channel covers: orange = Claude (David's rule), green/black = ChatGPT, blue-purple gradient = Gemini, blue = Meta. (Product colours from my own knowledge, UNVERIFIED here; check against the real logos already in the repo.)
- Highest contrast pair with black: 16.6 : 1 (WCAG ratio, computed by me from the hex values). It does not work on white (1.27 : 1), so the rule is: **yellow is always a fill behind black, never text or thin lines on white.**
- It has a meaning: a highlighter. The brand idea becomes "the part that actually works, highlighted".
- It must stay lemon-leaning. If it drifts toward amber it starts to read as orange/Claude.
- Risk: yellow + black is also Snapchat, and Vox uses yellow. Fallback accent if David dislikes yellow: electric blue `#2D5BFF` (cooler, closer to Mav's blue avatar; risk: Meta/Gemini territory).

### Concept A: "The Tick Ligature" (letters that contain the check)
- **Idea:** the check is not pasted next to the name, it is INSIDE the letters. In a heavy geometric sans, A is "/\" and W is "\/\/". Joined with shared strokes, AW becomes one zigzag of five diagonal strokes: up, down, up, down, up. The last two strokes (short down, long up) ARE a check mark. So the monogram reads as "AW" and as "tick" at once.
- **Construction:** canvas 1000 x 1000. Five straight strokes, all at 62 degrees from the baseline, uniform thickness 120. Baseline at y = 760. Strokes 1-4 reach cap height at y = 300. Stroke 5 (the check's long arm) overshoots to y = 170 and leans slightly more (58 degrees) so it reads as a flick. All joints mitred sharp at the top, flat-cut at the baseline; no round caps anywhere (this alone separates it from the v1 check). The A's crossbar is a horizontal bar, thickness 90, between strokes 1 and 2 at y = 590, and it is the ONLY yellow element (a highlighter bar); it extends 60 past stroke 1 on the left, as if swiped by hand. Total width about 720, centred, inside the central 70% of the circle.
- **Colours:** black `#0B0B0C` circle, white `#FFFFFF` zigzag, yellow `#FFE600` crossbar. Inverse: white circle, black zigzag, yellow bar.
- **At 32 px:** the zigzag is about 23 px wide, each stroke about 4 px, gaps about 2 px. Below 48 px the crossbar is removed and stroke 5 turns yellow instead, so the reading is "white zigzag ending in a yellow tick". Two flat colours on black, one silhouette.
- **Wordmark:** ACTUALLY WORKS in **Archivo Black**, or Archivo at width 110-125 ExtraBold (SIL Open Font License 1.1; licence checked in `github.com/google/fonts/ofl/archivoblack` and `/ofl/archivo`, both `license: "OFL"`). Caps, two lines, WORKS on a yellow bar. The same sharp cut terminals as the mark.
- **Strength:** typographic, serious, closest to the Vox family. **Weakness:** five strokes is near the limit at comment size; a zigzag can read as "M/W" or a pulse line to someone who does not know the name. It needs the 32 px test before anyone falls in love with it.

### Concept B: "The Highlight" (one swipe across one letter)
- **Idea:** from David's Q4: "for any question about AI, we have the answer". A = Actually = Answer. One huge A, and one real highlighter swipe across it: the thing that actually works, marked. The swipe, not the letter, is the ownable part, and it becomes the brand's device everywhere (one marked word in every headline, which is also what Eilon does with red).
- **Construction:** canvas 1000 x 1000, black circle. A white capital A, very heavy, no crossbar: an apex-up wedge with a triangular notch cut from the bottom. Outer shape: apex at (500, 200), feet at (215, 800) and (785, 800), feet flat-cut, each leg 190 thick at the base; inner notch apex at (500, 520). Across it, where a crossbar would be, a yellow swipe: a parallelogram 760 wide x 150 high, centred at y = 600, rotated 6 degrees counter-clockwise, running past both legs (from x = 120 to x = 880). The swipe sits ON TOP of the A. Its two short ends are irregular (drawn once from a scan of a real marker stroke on paper, then fixed forever), its long edges nearly straight. That real-marker edge is what keeps it from looking generated.
- **Colours:** black `#0B0B0C`, white `#FFFFFF`, yellow `#FFE600`. On white backgrounds: black A, yellow swipe behind it.
- **At 32 px:** a white triangle with a yellow band on a black disc: two shapes, three flat colours, readable as a blob of colour before it is readable as a letter. The rough ends are replaced by clean angled cuts below 64 px.
- **Wordmark:** "Actually Works" in **Bricolage Grotesque** ExtraBold (SIL OFL 1.1; `github.com/google/fonts/ofl/bricolagegrotesque`, `license: "OFL"`), sentence case, tight; the word "Actually" sits on the yellow swipe. Bricolage has slightly odd, hand-cut letter shapes, which answers Roni's "more unique fonts".
- **Strength:** simplest of the three, strongest colour signature, gives every video a reusable device (the swipe). **Weakness:** "letter A + accent bar" is a crowded field (many brands are a single A); the ownability rests on the yellow swipe and on consistency, not on the letter.

### Concept C: "The Wink" (a face for a faceless channel)
- **Idea:** all four reference brands are a face, and Juan's whole signature is an expression. So give the channel a face without showing a person: two eyes on a disc, where one eye is a solid dot and the other eye IS a check mark, a wink that says "trust me, this one works". The check stops being a generic icon because it is now an eye. And the face can react: in a video where the thing fails, the eye becomes an X; while testing, both eyes are dots looking at the screen; at the payoff, the wink. That matches the channel's promise (we test, and we show what failed) and gives every reel a 0.5 s end signature.
- **Construction:** canvas 1000 x 1000. Disc: yellow `#FFE600`, full bleed (the avatar circle itself is the head). Left eye: solid black circle, diameter 150, centre (345, 470). Right eye: a black check, short arm from (560, 470) to (625, 545), long arm from (625, 545) to (790, 330), stroke 78, **flat-cut ends, sharp mitred corner** (not the round-cap v1 glyph), so the long arm flicks up past the top of the left eye like a raised eyebrow. No nose. Mouth: optional and only above 200 px: one short flat black bar, 150 x 44, centre (500, 700), tilted 5 degrees (a smirk). Eyes sit slightly above centre and slightly right, so the face looks "at" the content beside it.
- **Colours:** yellow disc, black features. Reversed version: black disc, white dot eye, yellow check eye. No third colour.
- **At 32 px:** a yellow disc with a dot and a tick: two black shapes of similar visual weight. The mouth is dropped. In a comment list a yellow disc is found before it is read. On a white page the yellow disc needs a 1.5% black outline ring or the edge fades (principle 7 above).
- **Wordmark:** ACTUALLY WORKS in **Archivo Black** or Bricolage Grotesque ExtraBold (both SIL OFL 1.1, checked as above); the wink stays a separate element to the left of the wordmark, at 1.4 x the cap height; it is not worked into any letter.
- **Strength:** the only concept that replaces what the references actually have (a face with an expression); animates naturally; works as a mascot, sticker and end-card; scores best on the doodle test (JUDGEMENT). **Weaknesses, said plainly:** it sits close to emoji (the wink emoji, a smiley), so the drawing must be clearly custom (flat cuts, the eyebrow flick, the off-centre gaze) or it reads as an emoji; a full yellow disc is close to Snapchat's colour; and a friendly face is less "serious business" if David later sells AI systems to companies (the black-disc version helps there).

### Which one (JUDGEMENT, David decides)
- For the stated goal (followers, very large reach, exciting tone): **C first**, because it is the only one that gives a faceless channel an expression, and it can be animated into the videos. **B** as the safe, cleanest option. **A** only if David wants a typographic, media-company feel.
- They are compatible: B's yellow swipe can be the headline device of the whole system whichever mark wins.
- Before any choice: draw all three at 320, 110, 40 and 32 px in a circle, on white and on black, next to five real avatars from an Instagram comment list (the four references' faces would do), and look at them at arm's length. That test, not the description, decides.
- Not checked: whether a similar mark already exists (no trademark or reverse-image search was done). Do that for the chosen concept before it goes on the profile.
