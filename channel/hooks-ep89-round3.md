# Episode 89, hook candidates, round 3 (10.11.2026)

Topic unchanged: one line makes ChatGPT ask 3 questions before it writes. Round 3 keeps only what the three judges rewarded in round 2: a REAL message on a REAL phone going to a REAL person every viewer has (mum, grandma, dad, best friend, family group, class group), with the pain or the laugh felt in one second. No props, no metaphors, no "you use it wrong", no "finds you well" as the joke on its own.

Status: all 30 are HYPOTHESES. None is measured and none has been read by the judges yet. My own expectation is at the bottom.

Rules applied: spoken line 4-8 words, present tense, plain English, ChatGPT in the first 4 words or its real logo on frame 0, none of prompt / OpenAI / tip / hack / trick / challenge / stop / paste, fix not revealed, max 3 lines of text on screen, a real phone in a real hand with the recipient's name readable, ONE thing that moves.

Honesty conditions for the build:
- Every "what ChatGPT wrote" screen is a staged dramatisation and carries a small "dramatisation" label, unless it is marked REAL RUN below; those must be our own real run shown as it came out, and the hook is dropped if the run does not give it.
- Judge B's trust point (astronaut cards): the staged text must be something ChatGPT plausibly writes from a short request ("Warm regards", "[Your Name]", "I hope this message finds you well", "please be advised", cheerful emoji). I kept to that. Hooks where plausibility is doubtful are marked RISK.
- Recipients' replies are staged; no real person's message is shown unless that person agreed (hook 12).
- No payoff says the result "is better". What we can show: with the line, it asks three questions first.

Format: (a) spoken first sentence, (b) frame 0, (c) family, (d) withheld + payoff, (e) who to send it to.

## My honest top 8

1. #21 "ChatGPT signs my text: 'Your Name.'": joins the two round-2 hooks that scored 78 from all ages; the bracket really happens, so it passes the trust test; one glance at "[Your Name]" under "With love" explains the video.
2. #16 "Grandma calls. She thinks I'm hacked.": every age has or had a grandma; the punchline is a reaction, the banner and buzz are real motion, and it is a story people tell at dinner.
3. #29 "ChatGPT's text is sent. Delete it. Fast.": the only one with real tension in frame 0 (blue ticks versus the thumb); no tip reel opens like this.
4. #18 "Best friend replies: 'Okay, ChatGPT.'": a reply people truly send; strongest for 17 and 28, weaker for the parent.
5. #8 "Scroll up. That's me. This is ChatGPT.": the picture does the work with no reading; plausible; weaker stake.
6. #10 "ChatGPT, who is this text for?": the best answer to judge B's "seen it" objection, and it matches the shape of our top reels; weak as a scroll-stopper (a chat screen) and depends on the real run.
7. #7 "ChatGPT sends confetti. Mum's day is awful.": a felt stake rather than a joke; needs two beats to read.
8. #15 "Mum replies: 'Who is this?'": simplest and clearest; close to what was already scored 75-78.

Which 3 could reach 90: #21, #16 and #29. Honest view: I do not expect a median of 90 from any of them. Round 2's best was 79, and these are refinements of the same idea, so my own estimate is 80-86 for those three on a text description, and a rendered frame has scored about 20 points lower than its description before. What could lift them is the build (a real hand, a readable name, the motion landing in the first half second), not the wording. Judge B's point also stands: the payoff is a known line, so a daily user's score is capped unless the first seconds show something new (#29 and #10 are the attempts at that). All scores are judgements, not measurements.
