# 10 top-of-funnel topic concepts, built on Roni's feedback (9.11.2026)

Read first: roni-feedback-2026-11-09.md, CLAUDE.md, hooks-guide.md (headings), winners-structure-2026-11-09.md, research-2026-11-09-hooks.md / -viral.md, references-2026-11-09.md, next90/existing-titles.txt (87 lines).
Labels: FACT = read in a live search on 9.11.2026 (URL given). UNCONFIRMED = could not confirm. JUDGEMENT = my score, not a measurement (scale: channel/score-calibration.md).
Design brief applied to all ten: top of funnel (a stranger gets it in under 3 s), about something already in the viewer's life, AI is the substance, one hook sentence of 9 words or fewer, spoken AND shown at frame 0, no screen recordings (Vox-style drawn/animated), something to save or send, a reason to watch twice. Roni's style asks (his points 3-6): hard sound effects, subtle zoom in/out, non-default display fonts per episode, animated kinetic titles, no Claude-looking look. Logos: real icons only, no caption under them (CLAUDE.md). Nothing here is run or built; some concepts need our own test run first (marked).
Why scores are held back: in the 9.11 validity test, frame-0 panel scores did NOT predict views. What I still rely on: the 4 best episodes (72/80/82/86) all name a product in the first words and are about what an AI DOES; and the viral rows in research-2026-11-09-hooks.md show broad everyday-object topics (phone, WhatsApp, Alexa) with hundreds of thousands to millions of views. That is correlation from survivors, not proof. None of the ten reaches the 95 bar with honesty; the best are 80-84 on hook. CLAUDE.md says do not build under an honest 85 overall: so the first three below are "build only if the pre-build check lifts them", and I say what lifts them.

## Ranked list

### 1. "Can you spot the AI photo? Most can't." (8 words)
- Working title: Real or AI? Microsoft tested 12,500 people.
- Frame 0 (Vox-style): two near-identical drawn photo frames side by side on a warm paper background, a big hand-drawn "?" wobbling between them, a stamp-style heavy display font (not Claude-like) reading "REAL or AI?", a ticking counter bar "62%" drawn as a coin that is almost a flip.
- Beats and sound effects: (1) 0-3 s hook, hard "thump" on the "?" slam, card pair zooms in 4%. (2) "Microsoft showed 12,500 people photos. They guessed right 62% of the time." coin-flip drawing, coin "ting", 62% counts up with a tick-tick. (3) Mini-quiz: two frames, "Which one is AI?", 1.5 s countdown ring, whoosh on each swipe, a tension riser; a reveal "thwack". (4) "People photos: 65%. Landscapes: 59 to 61%." bar chart grows with a rising sweep, then a "Tip" card: look at hands/text/background (only tips from the cited study or labelled as general advice). (5) "Send this to the person who says they can always tell." end card; the answer to frame 0 is shown again with the AI tell circled (reason to watch twice: the first quiz answer is revealed only at the end, so a viewer rewatches to look for the tell).
- Bigger-audience reason: everyone has seen a fake photo; zero AI knowledge needed; it is a game, not a lesson.
- Claim source: FACT, 9.11.2026: PC Gamer https://pcgamer.com/software/ai/microsoft-study-suggests-folks-cant-tell-the-difference-between-real-and-ai-generated-images-about-62-percent-of-the-time-can-you-do-any-better and PetaPixel https://petapixel.com/2025/07/30/a-little-over-50-of-people-can-recognize-ai-images-from-real-photos : Microsoft AI for Good Lab, 62% correct, 12,500+ participants, ~287,000 evaluations (sources differ: 187,000 in some outlets), 65% on people, 59-61% landscapes. The authors say it likely overestimates today's ability. Never state "62% wrong".
- Nearest existing title: #54 "The video call might not have a real face on it - here's the test that catches most of them" (different: a live video-call scam, not photo guessing).
- Risk: the images in the quiz must be real/AI pairs we can license (never invented: use our own AI-generated images made now, labelled, and real photos we own or public-domain). Quiz images must be verified as what we claim.
- Scores (JUDGEMENT): hook 82, virality 80, overall 79; predicted views ~800. I believe it anyway because a quiz makes the viewer do something in the first 3 s, a mechanism none of our weaker episodes had; if the blind reader says the subject is not clear at frame 0, rewrite the hook.

### 2. "A detector called the Declaration of Independence AI!" (8 words)
- Working title: The AI detector that failed on 1776.
- Frame 0: a drawn parchment scroll with a red rubber stamp "98% AI" slamming down on it, display serif font cracking in half; quill pen drawn mid-air.
- Beats and SFX: (1) hook, stamp "KA-CHUNK" + paper shake. (2) "Teachers use AI detectors on student essays." school-desk drawing, bell. (3) The test: same text into two detectors: one says 98% AI (ZeroGPT), another says human (GPTZero), animated meter needles swinging opposite, "bzzt" vs "ding". (4) "Stanford: 7 detectors called 61% of non-native English essays AI." essay stack with red stamps, rising dread drone. (5) "If someone ever says your work looks like AI, show them this." save card with 3 plain steps (keep drafts / version history, ask what tool, ask for a human review). Reason to rewatch: the two needles disagree, viewer replays to see which is which.
- Bigger-audience reason: every student, parent and writer is touched; fear of being falsely accused.
- Claim source: FACT, 9.11.2026: Decrypt https://decrypt.co/286121/ai-detectors-fail-reliability-risks (ZeroGPT 97.93% AI on the Declaration; GPTZero 89% human; QuillBot 100% human) and Stanford HAI https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ai-detectors-biased-against-non-native-english-writers (61.3% average false-positive rate on TOEFL essays, 7 detectors, 91 essays). Results depend on the tool: do NOT say "AI detectors say" without naming ZeroGPT; re-run it ourselves before shipping (live detector output can change). A blog citing a 0.5% 2026 figure is from a vendor and is not Stanford: UNCONFIRMED, not used.
- Nearest existing title: #53 "Software reads your resume before a person does" (different: hiring screening).
- Risk: accuracy of the named tool today (must re-run); political-looking text is fine, no claim about the document's authorship.
- Scores: hook 80, virality 78, overall 77; predicted ~700. Lift: have the live re-run result on screen as a drawn card.

### 3. "AI can hear your password as you type it." (9 words)
- Working title: Your keyboard has a sound. AI can read it.
- Frame 0: a drawn laptop keyboard with sound waves rising from each key like a spectrogram, each wave forming a letter, bold condensed display type, a pink "LISTENING" light.
- Beats and SFX: (1) hook, a single mechanical key-click with reverb tail on the word "type". (2) "Researchers trained AI on the sound of 36 keys." keys light up with a click each. (3) "From a phone next to the laptop: 95% right. Over a Zoom call: 93%." two dials fill. (4) The catch said honestly: "It needs malware to listen, and it was trained on the same keyboard." viewer relaxes then tightens. (5) Save card: "3 fixes": password manager, typing style, background noise (the paper's own suggestions); send line "Send this to someone who types passwords on calls". Rewatch: the spectrogram letters spell the word before the voice says it.
- Bigger-audience reason: everyone types passwords; wow/fear; no AI vocabulary.
- Claim source: FACT, 9.11.2026: https://darkreading.com/attacks-breaches/ai-model-can-replicate-password-listening-to-keystrokes , https://www.techspot.com/news/99709-researchers-develop-ai-can-log-keystrokes-acoustically-92.html : Durham/Surrey/Royal Holloway, arXiv preprint Aug 2023 (not peer reviewed), 95% with a phone 17 cm away, 93% Zoom, 92% Skype; needs a nearby microphone; falls sharply on an untrained keyboard. Some outlets blur Zoom and phone numbers: use the right one.
- Nearest existing title: #44 "AI can already read your private messages" (different: messages vs sound).
- Risk: 2023 lab result, not an attack seen in the wild; the hook must not imply it is happening to you now. Say "in a lab".
- Scores: hook 80, virality 76, overall 76; predicted ~600.

### 5. "Your mom's voice on the phone can be fake." (9 words)
- Working title: The one word that beats a voice clone.
- Frame 0: a drawn phone, caller "Mom" with a cracked-mask face behind the screen, a waveform that turns into a barcode, big warning-red italic font, siren-like double beep.
- Beats and SFX: (1) hook, a phone ring with a distorted glitch on "fake". (2) "A few seconds of audio from a video can be enough to copy a voice." waveform copies itself, "clone" stamp. (3) "The call: 'I'm in trouble, send money now.'" countdown clock ticking faster. (4) the fix: "Hang up. Call back on the number you already have." phone hangs up, relief chime. (5) Save card: "Pick a family safe word. Never post it." send line "Send this to your mom." Rewatch: the safe word is revealed as a blank card the viewer must fill in themselves (a loop back to the "?" on the face).
- Bigger-audience reason: parents and grandparents; most emotional of the ten; highest sendability to a named person.
- Claim source: FACT (secondary), 9.11.2026: FTC family-emergency-scam guidance, consumer.ftc.gov (page text returned only in fragments); Tom's Guide https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/got-a-frantic-phone-call-from-a-loved-one-why-you-should-hang-up-immediately-to-avoid-this-terrifying-ai-voice-scam ; Spokesman-Review https://spokesman.com/stories/2023/oct/23/should-you-have-a-family-safe-word-against-ai-voic . UNCONFIRMED: that the FTC itself uses the words "safe word": check consumer.ftc.gov before citing it as official. No invented loss numbers.
- Nearest existing title: #38 "Ten seconds of your voice is all AI needs to clone it now" and #42 (phone AI that catches a scammer). Closest of all ten: it overlaps #38; the new angle is the family fix.
- Risk: topic partly covered (#38); scam topics are saturated; the "hang up and call back" fix is advice, not a test.
