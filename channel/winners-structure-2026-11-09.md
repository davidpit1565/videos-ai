## (b) What the four share, and what they do not

FACT, shared by all four (from the files):
1. A named AI product in the first words of the spoken hook (ChatGPT x3, Claude x1). All four are about what a chat AI SAYS, not about a tool's settings.
2. The subject of the sentence is the AI's behaviour (it told, it caved, it did not cave, it got dumb), not a feature or a how-to.
3. Plain words, short sentences, an exclamation on the hook line (82, 80, 72, 86).
4. Short: 27.5, 28.2, 36.8, 50.2 s.
5. The AI's real wording appears, quoted (72, 80, 86 the real chat answers; 82 the real postmortem dates).
6. The ending is a send-to-someone or comment prompt, not a "follow for more" (82 asks for a comment; 72 and 80 "send this to...").
7. The reach came from non-followers and did not convert: 72, 80, 82 had 94-97% non-followers; ep 86 gave +1 follower, 0 shares; 80 had 0 follows, 1 send.
8. Weak fit to the "setup path" idea: none of them leaves a long how-to; the proof is short.

FACT, NOT shared (so do not claim these as the pattern):
- Not all are first person (82 is not; 80 is not either).
- Not all use a real screen recording: 82 and 86 are drawn; 80 reuses frames from ep 79 plus drawn Settings; only 72 is a full-bleed recording.
- Not all are tests: 82 is a sourced list.
- Hook comprehension: ep 86 failed Miki's first-second test and still won (views do not prove comprehension).

What the weaker episodes lack (FACT from the numbers, HYPOTHESIS on the cause):
- 85 (189 views, "ChatGPT might think you're a teen") and 81 (189, "yes-man" fix) and 84 (202), 87 (about 90 at 1 h) are explainers or fixes: "what the company says, where the switch is". The winners show an AI doing something odd in a scene and let the viewer judge. HYPOTHESIS: the winners are watchable as a story with a twist (it said no, then yes; it promised, then told); the weaker ones have no scene.
- 73 (all drawn, no recording) did worse than 72 by David's account (HYPOTHESIS: a drawn demo without a real answer has no proof).
- The weaker ones usually have no second AI or role to compare against; 86 had three (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude) and a role switch.
- Selection note: n=4 winners out of 86 episodes; the median is about 204 views, and reach also depends on posting hour and luck. Two of the four (72, 80) are the same sub-genre (ChatGPT agrees with you), so independent evidence is smaller than "four".

HYPOTHESES (untested): (1) "an AI behaves differently when you tell it X" gives a one-sentence premise a stranger understands; (2) the result is shown before or at the same moment as the setup, so the viewer has a reason to see the proof; (3) a twist at the end (Claude refused, the AI did not cave) is the reason to stay; (4) a named other person ("someone pulling an all-nighter") makes it sendable.

## (c) The reusable template: "an AI behaves differently when you tell it X"

Length target 25-35 s (our winners: 27-37 s except 80). Every beat needs something moving on screen (alive rule).
0.0-2.0 s. HOOK, spoken and on screen at frame 0: "<Product> <does the odd thing> when I <tell it X>!" Product named in word one; X is something the viewer could say too. Frame 0 shows the product's real logo (icon only, no caption) plus the typed X in big words, and the first reply already starting to appear. Blind-reader check required (CLAUDE.md 8.11): "what is this about? would you stop?".
2.0-5.0 s. THE SAME QUESTION, TWO WAYS: show the question once without X ("I ask: <plain question>") and the answer in one line, then once with X. Show both chats side by side or one after another, answers quoted big and lit word by word as the voice reads them.
5.0-14.0 s. THE DIFFERENCE, in the AI's own words: the real answer text for version A, then for version B, with one ring drawn on the part that changed (ep 72 style: white headline bar plus ONE ring). The voice reads the key sentence; no silence.
14.0-20.0 s. THE TWIST / SECOND PRODUCT: "And <second AI>? It did the opposite" or "I pushed once more". One more result so the viewer has a score (2 of 3, 1 of 3).
20.0-26.0 s. THE TAKEAWAY the viewer can CHECK in 30 seconds: one card with the exact two messages to type (screenshot-able; this is the SAVE item). Not a settings path unless the test shows a setting.
26.0-30.0 s. SEND LINE naming a person: "Send this to someone who <does X>." Then the locked "The setup's in the link in bio." only if the episode really has a setup page. (The tomorrow line applies as per CLAUDE.md.)
Caption: first line restates the video; the exact messages typed; what happened per product with plan/mode/date; what is NOT claimed (one run, one account); try-it line; real source links; the Anthropic/Claude disclosure when Claude is involved.
Hard rules for the template: only run it where we really ran it (never staged); the outcome is whatever happened ("It did not cave" or "It caved"); real logos; nothing covers the answer; safe box y 269-1248.

### C1. Gemini changes its answer when I say my age
- Hook: "I told Gemini I'm 14. It changed its answer!"
- First frame: Gemini logo, the sentence "I'm 14" typed in a bubble, two answers sliding in side by side with one line ringed.
- Test: same question in two fresh chats ("Is it okay to skip breakfast before an exam?" or similar low-stakes health/study question), first plain, then with "I'm 14". Show the real answers. Run by: David on the Gemini app (personal account; temporary chat not available on work accounts per ep 86 notes). Outcome unknown until run; if the answers do not differ, there is no video.
- In existing titles? No. Closest: #85 "Does ChatGPT think you're a teen?" (ChatGPT, explainer, 189 views) and #83 Gemini chats (privacy). Different angle.
- Scores: H 80, V 72, Val 62, O 72. Plausible ceiling 80; 85+ only if the difference is dramatic and quotable.

### C2. Google search AI answer agrees with both sides of a question
- Hook: "I Googled it two ways. Google's AI agreed with both!"
- First frame: Google logo, two search bars stacked: "is <X> good for you" and "is <X> bad for you", both AI answers visible with a red tick on each "yes".
- Test: pick one everyday, low-stakes, checkable topic (for example a food or a phone habit); two searches differing only in the leading word; show the real AI Overview text for both. Run by: David on a phone or laptop with AI Overviews. Needs live check that AI Overviews appear for him (Belgium, language) and that it really agrees with both; if not, no video.
- In existing titles? No (#49 "Google's AI answer at the top of your search is wrong 1 in 10 times" is about accuracy, a different angle; do not claim a number).
- Scores: H 84, V 80, Val 70, O 78. Best of the six for a stranger to verify in 30 seconds on a product everyone uses. Plausibly 85 on the overall only if the contradiction is clean and the topic is chosen to be safe; I would not call it 90.

### C3. Be rude to an AI vs say please (Gemini)
- Hook: "I was rude to Gemini. Look at the difference!" (only if there is one)
- First frame: Gemini logo, two chat bubbles: "please, could you..." and "just answer, you useless bot", answers as two cards.
- Test: one question, three tones (polite, neutral, rude), the real answers side by side; measure length and what is dropped. Run by: David (Gemini app). It may show no difference: then the honest video is "no difference", which is a weaker hook.
- In existing titles? No.
- Scores: H 76, V 70, Val 55, O 66. Not 85.

### C5. Meta AI in WhatsApp: "keep this between us"
- Hook: "I told Meta AI in WhatsApp to keep it private. Who else sees it?"
- First frame: WhatsApp logo, a group chat with "@Meta AI" typed, an eye icon over the group members.
- Test: in a group chat ask Meta AI something with a made-up secret, then show what the other members see (the mention and the answer are in the group); also ask in a one-to-one chat and show that. Run by: David with a second phone or a friend's view; no real secrets. Whether Meta AI is available in WhatsApp in his country, and how it handles group mentions, is UNVERIFIED and must be checked live first. The test result is a fact about where the message goes, not about the AI "telling".
- In existing titles? No (#58 WhatsApp AI photo redraw is a different feature; #60 Instagram AI chat is about ads).
- Scores: H 80, V 78, Val 72, O 76. High felt stake (a group chat) and sendable to one named person; depends entirely on what the real test shows.

### C6. "I told Gemini I'm broke. Then I told it I'm rich."
- Hook: "I told Gemini I'm broke. Then I said I'm rich!"
- First frame: Gemini logo, two phones: a laptop question with the prices in the two answers circled.
- Test: ask the same shopping question ("Which laptop should I buy for school?") in two fresh chats, once with "I'm on a tiny budget" and once with "money is no problem"; show the real recommendations and whether the "broke" answer steers to worse or cheaper options. Run by: David (Gemini app), prices checked on the real shop pages if they are quoted.
- In existing titles? No.
- Scores: H 80, V 74, Val 68, O 74. Personal and easy to send to someone buying something; it can also come out as "of course it changes", which is dull.
