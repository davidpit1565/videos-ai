03The thing you're missing

You said the goal is 1,000,000 followers. Based on the model you're copying, that's the wrong target — and it's roughly twenty times harder than the right one.

Here are the current market rates for newsletter sponsorship. AI is the premium category right now because sponsor demand is high and there isn't enough inventory:

NicheCPM (per 1,000 subscribers, per send)
General newsletters$7 – $50
Technology & dev tools$60 – $150
Marketing & B2B SaaS$80 – $200
AI-focused, 30k+ subscribers$140 – $220

The arithmetic that should change your plan

10,000 AI-niche subscribers × $100 CPM = $1,000 per sponsored send
2 sends per week × 4 slots sold per month ≈ $4,000 / month

30,000 subscribers × $150 CPM = $4,500 per send → $18,000+ / month

Now compare: 1,000,000 Instagram followers earns roughly $0 directly. Instagram's creator payouts are small, inconsistent, and repeatedly discontinued. A million followers is worth something only when it feeds somewhere that does convert.

Change the target

10,000 email subscribers beats 1,000,000 followers — for revenue, for stability, and for how hard it is to get there. Followers are rented from a platform that can change the rules tomorrow. A list is owned. If Instagram deleted your account tonight, the list survives and the revenue survives with it.

Keep the 1M number as a vanity milestone if you like. Don't run the business on it.

The other four things you're missing, in order of how much they'll cost you:

  1. There is nothing to buy and nowhere to sign up. If a video went viral tomorrow you'd capture nothing from it. Fix this before you post, not after.
  2. Production was never the bottleneck. You automated the cheap part. Distribution and offer are the expensive parts and no tool solves them.
  3. "Actually works" means you have to actually test. Every setup you publish must be one you personally ran. That's the real recurring time cost, and it can't be automated — it's also the entire reason anyone would follow you rather than the fifty accounts reposting the same tips.
  4. You haven't decided Hebrew or English. English gives 20× the market and pays 5–10× the CPM. Hebrew gives a small market with almost no competition and much warmer service leads. Both are valid. Doing both badly is not.

Your voice is not your face

You can stay completely anonymous and still narrate. Recording your own voice costs nothing, takes about 20 minutes per video, and is the single largest quality jump available to you right now — bigger than any visual change I can make. A phone in a quiet room beats every TTS engine on the market.

If you truly won't record: ElevenLabs, roughly $5–22/month, is the fallback. It's clearly better than what we're using. Everything else in the pipeline stays identical — only the audio file changes.

On your "diversify by personas" idea — the instinct is right but the axis is wrong. Don't diversify by inventing fake humans; that multiplies your workload and divides your trust. Diversify by format instead, under one identity:

  • The Setup — screen recording, 45–75s. Your bread and butter.
  • The Teardown — "I gave this agent my inbox for a week, here's what broke." Results, not tutorials.
  • The Comparison — two tools, one decision, three minutes. Highest-converting format in this niche.
  • The Honest One — what doesn't work. Your signature, and the reason people trust the other three.

05Seven ways this makes money, ranked

Ranked by realistic money per hour of your time in the first year. All figures are industry-typical ranges, not promises — treat them as arithmetic to check, not income to expect.

01

Done-for-you AI systems for businesses

Opportunity: highRisk: low
Time to first $2–6 weeks
Realistic Y1$2k–15k/mo
Needs audience?No
CeilingYour hours

You already build AI systems — that's how this whole project started. Small businesses will pay $1,500–8,000 for one working automation. The content isn't the product here; it's proof you can do it. Two clients pay more than 100,000 followers will for a long time.

How exactly: put one line in your bio — "I build these for businesses" — and a booking link. Every video is a portfolio piece. Answer every DM. That's the entire funnel.

Why it's first: it works at zero followers, it funds everything else, and it makes your content better because you're solving real problems instead of guessing at them.

02

Newsletter sponsorships

Opportunity: highRisk: medium
Time to first $4–8 months
Realistic Y1$0–4k/mo
Needs audience?~5k subs
CeilingVery high

The model you're copying, and the reason section 03 exists. Sponsors pay per send, so revenue scales with list size and doesn't require you to sell anything to your readers.

5,000 subs × $80 CPM = $400/send · 8 sends/month, 2 sold = $800/mo
20,000 subs × $140 CPM = $2,800/send · 4 sold/month = $11,200/mo

How exactly: start the list on day one — Beehiiv or Kit, free tier. Every video ends with "full instruction in the newsletter". At ~2,000 subscribers, list yourself on Paved and Sponsorgap and make a one-page media kit. Sponsors come to you in this niche; you rarely have to chase.

03

Affiliate revenue from AI tools

Opportunity: mediumRisk: medium
Time to first $1–3 months
Realistic Y1$200–3k/mo
Needs audience?Small
CeilingMedium

n8n, Make, Zapier, Lindy, Notion and most AI tools pay 20–30% recurring. Your content is already tool-led, so this costs you nothing extra.

How exactly: only ever link tools you genuinely use in a video. Disclose every time — it's legally required in most markets and it's also the reason people believe you.

The trap

Affiliate money quietly rewrites what you cover. The moment you're choosing episodes by commission instead of usefulness, the "actually works" promise is dead and so is the channel. Cap it: never more than one affiliate link per video, and never recommend a tool you'd not use unpaid.
